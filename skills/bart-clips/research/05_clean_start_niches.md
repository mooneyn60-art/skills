# 05: Which niche to start a new original Shorts channel in (researched 2026-10-15)

**Labels.** **[M]** means measured: a number I pulled myself from vidIQ on 2026-10-15, or a figure quoted from a cited source. **[I]** means inference: my own judgment, built on the measured data. **[U]** means an unverified third-party claim.

**vidIQ credits.** The balance was 6,033 before and 5,903 after, so **130 credits were spent** against a budget of 600. Calls cost 5 credits each, and rising-mode calls that returned nothing cost 0.

**How to read the keyword figures:**
- "Searches" is vidIQ's `estimatedMonthlySearch`, which covers all countries.
- "Growth" is `searchDemandGrowthPct`: the current monthly searches compared with the figure 30 days earlier.
- "Comp" is vidIQ's 0–100 competition score. Lower is easier.
- "VPH trend" is the change in topic-level views-per-hour from 2026-09-17 to 2026-10-14.

**How to read the channel figures.** Channel numbers come from `vidiq_channel_search` with `breakoutChannel: true`, language English, and channel created on or after 2025-10-15. "Est $/mo" is vidIQ's estimated earnings, which is a model output, not the channel's real revenue.

## TL;DR

**#1: car-buying insider Shorts, "the salesman tells you what the dealer won't."** The owner works as a car salesman, and that real expertise is the moat. It is the only candidate where three things line up:
- **Rising demand [M]:**
  - "car buying tips" +165.9%
  - "buying a new car" +77.8%
  - "car dealer" +86.4%
  - "car salesman" +27.0%
- **Low-to-moderate competition [M]:** competition scores of 37–50, against 58–72 for the "entertainment" niches.
- **The strongest policy defense [I]:** the content is first-person, expert and original. That is exactly what YouTube's reused/inauthentic and Oct-2026 originality rules reward.

The agent can do about 80% of the work: script, data, rendered "deal-sheet / payment-math" visuals, and editing. The owner's 10–30 seconds of recorded voice per Short does the rest, and that is the part that makes it his.

**Runner-up: "weird/creepy history oddities."** Demand is huge and rising, and new channels have broken out fast. It loses on originality risk and moat.

## Monetization reality check (applies to every niche)

- **Shorts RPM is low everywhere.**
  - AIR Media-Tech measured 274 channels / 3,044 channel-months (updated 2026-09-17): Shorts RPM runs **$0.02–$1.48** across niches. That is about **3–14% of long-form RPM**; the higher end is mostly music, via Content ID. [M]
  - The ShortSync estimate puts finance/business/insurance at the top (**$0.15–0.25+**), then tech ($0.12–0.22), then education ($0.08–0.15). Entertainment is $0.04–0.08. [U, unsourced aggregate]
  - Automotive isn't broken out by any source I found. Car *buying* sits next to auto-finance and insurance advertisers, so I place it in the finance tier. [I]
- **YPP thresholds** (from file 04):
  - Today: 1,000 subs + 10M Shorts views in 90 days.
  - From **2027-02-01**: 20M views to join, and 10M per 90 days to keep earning Shorts revenue.
  - A pure-Shorts channel is unlikely to earn meaningful ad revenue in year one. The realistic money paths are:
    - long-form companions (car-buying long-form behaves like finance; the faceless Auto Insider made 74 long videos)
    - fan funding at 500 subs
    - TikTok Creator Rewards (videos **≥1 minute** and original [U, riverside.com])
    - affiliate links: credit-score, insurance-comparison and car-buying-service programs [I]
    - for the car niche only: **lead generation**. Adrian The Car Guy and EliTalksAutos both sell "I'll help you buy your car" services in their descriptions [M]. This is the only path that pays before YPP.
- **AI/faceless enforcement 2026:**
  - Neal Mohan's 2026-01-21 letter made reducing "AI slop" a 2026 priority, building on systems against "low-quality, repetitive content." [M, via Tubefilter / Variety coverage]
  - YouTube's inauthentic-content examples include "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production without adding the creator's original, authentic insights." [quoted via OutlierKit / SubSub, U]
  - Vendors report faceless channels being demonetized while still getting views. [U]
  - Takeaway [I]: AI voice and AI tooling are tolerated, but **template sameness and having no human perspective are what get punished**. The owner's real voice and expertise is the best insurance available.

## Per-niche detail and example ideas

### 1. Car-buying insider (RECOMMENDED)
- **Demand [M]:**

  | Keyword | Searches/mo | Growth | Comp |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | car buying tips | 11,409 | +165.9% | 50 |
  | buying a car | 29,781 | +39.9% | — |
  | buying a used car | 6,205 | +87.9% | — |
  | how to negotiate a car deal | 8,134 | flat | 52.9 |
  | car salesman salary | 4,755 | — | 23.5 |
  | how to hold gross in car sales | 4,859 | — | 23.5 |
  | sales training | 153,906 | +75.3% | — |

  The low-competition, insider-flavored long tails (car salesman salary, how to hold gross) suggest a second audience of other salespeople.
- **Breakouts [M]:**
  - Dealership IRL went from creation (2026-07-17) to 554k views and 54 Shorts with subs +62% in 30 days.
  - Carzooka (created 2026-05-30) averages 13.3k views per video from just 17 uploads.
  - Shorts outliers: "Struggles of a salesman" (Mike Makes Car Deals, 491 subs) 3,864,231 views; "Undercover Salesman Ep2 pt2" (2,030 subs) 219,970 views at 13,219 VPH; "Always pull a … report when buying a car" (1,710 subs) 305,158.
- **The gap [I]:**
  - Most of the competition is **dealership-branded** channels: Kia of Columbia, Sarat Ford, Clark Nissan. They get views but few subs: Clark Nissan has 35 subs at a 16k average. Viewers don't subscribe to an ad.
  - A **buyer-side "salesman confesses"** brand with a consistent host voice and data visuals is under-supplied.
  - Joe-Bartolozzi-style deadpan commentary fits naturally: "this is the part where I'm supposed to say…"
- **Path to 1,000 subs [I]:**
  - 1–2 Shorts/day for 60–90 days, with a series format ("Dealer Trick #N").
  - Every Short ends on a repeatable promise ("follow so you never overpay").
  - Long-form 8–12 minute "full deal breakdown" videos 1–2 times a month carry search traffic. vidIQ shows US demand in long-tail search terms.
  - Benchmarks: Auto Insider reached 2,490 subs and Motor Minds Hub 1,010 subs within ~9 months on long-form alone [M].
- **Automation [I]:**
  - The agent writes scripts from owner bullet points and pulls public data (average transaction price, APR by credit tier, dealer fees by state).
  - It renders buyer's-order / four-square / amortization visuals in HTML or Python and cuts to ffmpeg.
  - VoiceStudio clones the **owner's own** voice for bulk narration, which is legitimate because it is his voice. He records the hook line and sign-off live.
  - Disclose synthetic voice where YouTube requires it (realistic altered content).
- **Policy and personal risk [I]:**
  - Stay anonymous or check the dealership's social-media policy.
  - Never show customer PII or real deal jackets.
  - Avoid naming his own store.
  - Add "not financial advice" to descriptions.
  - Note that the FTC's CARS Rule was vacated by the 5th Circuit in Jan 2025. That is from memory, not re-verified here, so present add-on and fee content as "how it works," not legal claims.
- **Example videos:**
  1. "The 4 boxes your salesman draws, and the one he's hiding" (four-square explained with a rendered worksheet)
  2. "Why I always ask 'what payment are you looking for?' (it's a trap)" (payment-packing math on screen)
  3. "I'm a car salesman: never buy these 3 add-ons in the finance office" (GAP and paint protection, with markup data)

### 2. Weird / creepy history oddities
- Demand and breakouts are in the table. The Past Port proves ~20s Shorts can reach 30M views from a new channel in 5 months [M], but it shows the **thin moat**: anyone can copy it.
- To make it original and defensible [I], use a recurring host bit: the owner's deadpan "deep thought" closer after each fact, plus code-rendered maps and timelines, not AI slideshow images.
- **Example videos:**
  1. "The 1518 dancing plague: 400 people danced for weeks, and nobody knows why"
  2. "Lizzie Borden was acquitted. Here's the one detail the jury never heard" (rides the +340% spike)
  3. "The Great Emu War: Australia lost a war to birds (the numbers)"

### 3. What-if / hypothetical science
- Demand is the largest and still rising (VPH +54%) [M]. Supply, though, is a wall of skeleton/stickman AI clones. 8 of the 15 breakout results are this pattern [M], and the clones convert views to subs at about 0.07% [M].
- Pick it only with a distinctive, non-template visual and voice identity.
- **Example videos:**
  1. "What if every car on Earth went electric tomorrow? (the grid math)"
  2. "What if you drove at the speed of light for one second?"
  3. "What if gas cost $20 a gallon? Here's what happens to used-car prices"

### 4. Internet mysteries & lore
- This is a long-form niche. The Net Explainer earns est $4,251/mo from 20 long videos with AI voice only [M], but Shorts lore channels at 19k–71k subs show est $9–40/mo [M].
- Main-keyword demand is falling (−32.7%). "reddit mysteries" (+238%) is the live sub-pocket [M].
- **Example videos:**
  1. "The Reddit user who solved a 30-year-old cold case from one photo"
  2. "What is the 'Cicada 3301' puzzle, and was it ever solved?"
  3. "The YouTube channel that uploaded for 10 years and then vanished"

### 5. True-crime "fatal mistake"
- The volume is massive, but the monetized winners are 45–60-minute long-form [M]. Shorts cut against both the ad-suitability and reused-footage rules.
- The owner's interest fits better as an occasional "car crime" crossover inside #1, for example: "the dealership embezzler who got caught by one missing title."
- **Example videos:**
  1. "He stole $2M from a dealership. One missing car title caught him"
  2. "The getaway car that was registered to the robber's mom"
  3. "How a $40 parking ticket solved the Son of Sam case"

### 6. Money / consumer psychology
- Keywords are big with low competition (money psychology, comp 41.5), but **no new channel in the sample broke 600 subs** [M]. That pattern shows the niche is full of generic AI clones.
- Fold it into #1 as "dealer psychology."
- **Example videos:**
  1. "Why the first car they show you is always the wrong one (anchoring)"
  2. "'This price is only good today': the scarcity trick, explained"
  3. "Why $499/month feels cheaper than $29,000"

### 7. Data rankings / comparisons
- Fully code-renderable, but the evidence says Shorts underperform: CompareIt's Shorts got 1,353 views in a year while its long-form got 2.16M [M].
- **Example videos:**
  1. "Most stolen cars in America, 2026 (NICB data)"
  2. "Which car brands lose value fastest (5-year depreciation)"
  3. "Average car payment by state"

### 8. Deep thoughts / shower thoughts
- "deep thoughts" is up +71.3% and US-heavy [M], but it is a tone, not a topic. Use it as the closer style in #1 and #2.
- **Example videos:**
  1. "You've never seen a new car's odometer at zero. Think about that"
  2. "Every car you'll ever drive is already built or designed"
  3. "Your car spends 95% of its life parked"

## #1 recommendation and why

**Start "car-buying insider" Shorts, cross-posted to TikTok and Reels, with long-form breakdowns as the money engine.**

1. **The moat is real expertise.** It is the only candidate where originality is built into the content. The Oct-2026 originality ranking and the reused/inauthentic policies (file 04) reward first-person perspective, and a working car salesman can't be templated by clone channels. [I, grounded in policy text from file 04]
2. **Rising, under-served demand [M]:** +28% to +166% growth on core terms, competition 37–53. The dominant supply is dealership ad-channels that get views but not subscribers.
3. **Proof that new channels can break out [M]:** Dealership IRL reached 554k views in ~3 months; Carzooka averages 13.3k views per video; a 491-sub channel's salesman Short reached 3.86M views.
4. **The best money ceiling of the candidates [U/I]:** car buying sits in the finance/insurance advertiser tier, the highest Shorts RPM tier ($0.15–0.25+). It also has non-YPP income from day one: lead-gen and affiliate (credit, insurance, car-buying services), $0 to start.
5. **It fits the agent pipeline.** Script, data, rendered deal-sheet visuals, ffmpeg, and his own cloned voice in VoiceStudio cover about 80% of the work. Recorded hook lines and an occasional on-camera face are cheap upgrades that strongly help both trust and policy standing. [I]

**Watch-outs:**
- Check the employer's social-media policy, or stay anonymous with no store name, no customer data, and no deal jackets.
- Disclose synthetic or cloned voice where required.
- Keep "not financial/legal advice" lines in descriptions.
- Measure engaged views and viewed-vs-swiped ratio, not public views (file 04).
