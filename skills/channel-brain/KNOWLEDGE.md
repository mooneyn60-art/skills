# Channel knowledge (seed)

Tested findings with numbers, one line each. Copy to skills/oh-meatball/KNOWLEDGE.md and keep it growing.
Seeded by TARS on 2026-10-24 from research/24 and research/42.

- **82% of views come from the Shorts feed** (4,603 of ~5,600, Oct 1-24). Shorts are the engine; long-form is not yet.
- **~60% swipe away in the first second** (1,820 engaged of 4,603). The people who stay watch 128% (they finish and rewatch). The opening is the problem, not the content.
- **Search is source #2** (886 views at 64% viewed, all Shorts). Titles phrased as the question people type win there.
- **Long-form: 5 views in 30 days, ~28 s watched.** Zero suggested traffic. Daily 11-minute episodes are not paying off yet.
- **0 subscribers from ~5,600 views.** Nothing recurring gives a reason to subscribe.
- **Short length:** best Shorts ran 27-49 s (river shark 769, goldfish 661); the 88 s lemming got 188. Outlier rivals run 17-30 s.
- **Character-led "weird true things" channels break out** (Mister Fabio 288x, Sam O'Nella compilations 1,618x); topic-led quizzes don't.
- **(10/29, public views) The numbered "Things Living In Your House" series is the steadiest format:** #1-#5 got 547-1,500 each; single animal facts ~500-1,000; case-file Shorts 1-431 (5 tries); long-forms 2-152. Source: RESEARCH_2026-10-29_GROWTH.md.
- **(10/29) Rival outliers don't lift a base:** Nostril Animation has one 4.2M Short; its other Shorts run 1.4K-25K. Series (Colossal Cranium "YES IT'S TRUE", 100K-1.5M) and sequels of hits (Paint Explainer 2.3M -> Pt. 2 736K) are what repeat.
- **(Galloway, opinion with case studies) Format rule:** a test that does 2x the channel average twice in a row is a winning format; 70%+ of growth comes from 1-2 formats. (2023 study, 5,400 Shorts: long-form 22.7 vs Shorts 16.9 subs per 10K views; 50-60 s Shorts averaged the most.)

- **(11/12, research 85, yt-dlp flat listings of 752 channels found through 100 searches) For long videos, the shape matters more than the topic, and one shape fits us.** Small channels (under 100K subscribers) that title videos "Why It Sucks to Be Born as a [animal]": 41% of those videos pass 10,000 views (147 videos, 26 channels) against 23% for all long videos of such channels; five small animated joke-voice channels (25K to 46K subscribers) run it as their whole channel. - **(11/12, research 84 and 86) What the second-model check keeps catching in pet and survival scripts:** "your cat/dog" claims from lab tests without "probably" or "may"; reaction words that carry a feeling or motive; safety lines missing their condition (play dead only once a surprised grizzly has knocked you down; bear spray only on a charging or attacking bear); numbers without their unit or year. 21 fixes in five Shorts, about 25 in two TikTok cuts, none of them a wrong safety line.
- **(11/12, research 85 addendum, the same 148 small-channel videos split by title) In the "born as" shape two title families carry every winner:** "Why It Sucks to Be (Born as) X" (106 videos, 21 channels, median 4,150 views, 41% over 10,000) and "Your Life as X" (29 videos, 5 channels, median 29,000, 59% over 10,000; 13 of them one channel's movie monsters). ## Verified platform facts (2026-11-10, primary sources; research 71; moved here from MASTER_ORDERS on 11/13)
- **YPP:** 1,000 subs + 4,000 qualified long-form hours in 12 months, or 10M qualified Shorts views in 90 days. Shorts-feed watch time doesn't count toward hours; loops don't count as qualified Shorts views. **From 2027-02-01 new applicants need 8,000 hours or 20M Shorts views** (checked on YouTube's help page). The 500-sub tier (fan funding and Shopping, no ads: 3,000 hours or 3M Shorts views) is unchanged.
- **Shorts ranking, official signals:** chose-to-view vs swiped, average view duration, average % viewed, likes, surveys. **No official stayed-to-watch benchmark exists** (the "70%" figure is folklore: compare our Shorts with each other). Each video is judged on its own; a weak upload doesn't penalize the channel and no posting cadence is required.
- **Views** count from the first frame since 2026-08-24 on all formats; the old definition is now "engaged views".
- **Related video on a Short:** set in Studio (needs advanced features); no documented API field.
- **"A/B test titles & thumbnails"** (was Test & Compare): up to 3 variants, desktop Studio, long-form only, winner by watch-time share.
- **Hype** needs YPP membership, so we can't use it yet.
- **Inauthentic-content policy:** the risk is sameness (same situation, same outcome, a template). A recurring-character series is fine when each video has its own concept. The synthetic-content label is required only for realistic content, and YouTube says it doesn't limit reach or earnings.
- **TikTok Creator Rewards:** 10K followers + 100K views in 30 days to join; pays only on original videos of 1 minute or more that reach 1,000 qualified For You views (a qualified view is a unique For You view watched 5+ s). TikTok says the AI label doesn't affect distribution.

- **(11/13, research 100, `oh-meatball/research/100-growth-deep-dive.md`: Windsor 10/14 to 11/13, yt-dlp listings, re-computed by a second model) The deep dive:** long videos earned 10.4 hours in 28 days at 0.91 minutes a view (best 1.21 on the 4:34 "3 Bugs"; the 10:17 video held 11%), so 4,000 hours is about 263,000 long views and only a breakout gets there. Shorts hand long videos at most 0.49% of their views. In Shorts the one real gap is topic (house bugs and spiders median 1,146, other animals 474, p 0.001; four older survival Shorts 1,124); hour, weekday, gap, number per day and title tricks showed nothing in about 166 comparisons. Subscribers come at 1.04 per 1,000 Shorts views (63 on 60,436; interval 0.73 to 1.41). A Short earns 85% of its views on day 0 and about 1% after day 2. Not measurable: impressions and click-through per video (Studio only), retention curves (vidIQ 11/24).
