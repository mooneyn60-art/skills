# Channel knowledge (seed)

Tested findings with numbers, one line each. Copy to skills/oh-meatball/KNOWLEDGE.md and keep it growing.
Seeded by TARS on 2026-10-24 from research/24 and research/42.

- **82% of views come from the Shorts feed** (4,603 of ~5,600, Oct 1-24). Shorts are the engine; long-form is not yet.
- **~60% swipe away in the first second** (1,820 engaged of 4,603). The people who stay watch 128% (they finish and rewatch). The opening is the problem, not the content.
- **Search is source #2** (886 views at 64% viewed, all Shorts). Titles phrased as the question people type win there.
- **Long-form: 5 views in 30 days, ~28 s watched.** Zero suggested traffic. Daily 11-minute episodes are not paying off yet.
- **0 subscribers from ~5,600 views.** Nothing recurring gives a reason to subscribe.
- **Short length:** best Shorts ran 27-49 s (river shark 769, goldfish 661); the 88 s lemming got 188. Outlier rivals run 17-30 s.
- **Character-led "weird true things" channels break out** (Mister Fabio 288x, Sam O'Nella compilations 1,618x); topic-led quizzes don't.
- **(10/29, public views) The numbered "Things Living In Your House" series is the steadiest format:** #1-#5 got 547-1,500 each; single animal facts ~500-1,000; case-file Shorts 1-431 (5 tries); long-forms 2-152. Source: RESEARCH_2026-10-29_GROWTH.md.
- **(10/29) Rival outliers don't lift a base:** Nostril Animation has one 4.2M Short; its other Shorts run 1.4K-25K. Series (Colossal Cranium "YES IT'S TRUE", 100K-1.5M) and sequels of hits (Paint Explainer 2.3M -> Pt. 2 736K) are what repeat.
- **(Galloway, opinion with case studies) Format rule:** a test that does 2x the channel average twice in a row is a winning format; 70%+ of growth comes from 1-2 formats. (2023 study, 5,400 Shorts: long-form 22.7 vs Shorts 16.9 subs per 10K views; 50-60 s Shorts averaged the most.)
- **(vidIQ, 448K new channels, 7/2026) Median new-channel Short ~1,350 views in 30 days, flat across the first 10; 48% hit a 10K Short within 10.** Our House series (547-1,500) is median, not a breakout yet.
- **(YouTube policy 1311392) Inauthentic-content risk is sameness:** same situation, same outcome, templated, shock-only or misleading. A recurring-character series is allowed if every episode has its own storyline. TTS isn't mentioned.
- **(11/11, Studio screenshot) 1,000 subscribers reached.** Last 28 days: 66.5K views, 152 watch hours (mostly Shorts feed, which does not count toward the 4,000). The teaser Short "The Bug You Squish Is Protecting You" did 1.3K views in its first 11.5 hours at 118.3% average viewed, rank 2 of 10: the "it's protecting you" promise holds on Shorts as well as in the long-forms.

- **(11/11, research 82, our own TikTok pages) TikTok labels the drawn videos "Comics & Cartoon, Anime" (19 of 20; median 340 views) and the old real-photo ones "Animals" or "Education" (median 615 with the first house centipede, n=10).** Both of the account's biggest posts (5,983 and 3,791) are in the second group. A lead for why search works and For You does not; 31 videos, not random, so not proof.
- **(11/12, research 83, yt-dlp flat listings of small channels under 100K subscribers) Wider topics have a higher ceiling, not a higher floor.** Share of small-channel uploads passing 10,000 views, long / Shorts: space and "what if" 35% / 6%; big dangerous animals 29% / 13%; pets 23% / 36%; food 24% / 3%; house bugs 12.5% / 3.5% (bug Shorts from 7 channels only). Our own bug Shorts (about 1,550 median) are above the typical small-channel Short in every family (medians of channel medians 106 to 1,400). Chapman 2023: 25.3% of Americans are afraid of insects and spiders, so "bugs limit the audience" is not shown. Small animated fact channels that grew (StickTory, Hour Seven, Jeremy Bearimy, PEANUT) put the viewer in the scene: "what if you", "why it sucked to be", "why your brain". Sample is small and leans to winners.
- **(11/12, research 85, yt-dlp flat listings of 752 channels found through 100 searches) For long videos, the shape matters more than the topic, and one shape fits us.** Small channels (under 100K subscribers) that title videos "Why It Sucks to Be Born as a [animal]": 41% of those videos pass 10,000 views (147 videos, 26 channels) against 23% for all long videos of such channels; five small animated joke-voice channels (25K to 46K subscribers) run it as their whole channel. Under 20K subscribers it is 8% (median 1,000 views). "Would you survive [animal]" has no small-channel base as a long video. Pet lists and "if your cat does X": 14 to 15%, no lift, all real footage. Winners in every shape run about 9 minutes (median 8.9; 8 to 12 is the most common bin). Sample found through search, so it leans to winners; no retention data.
- **(11/12, research 84 and 86) What the second-model check keeps catching in pet and survival scripts:** "your cat/dog" claims from lab tests without "probably" or "may"; reaction words that carry a feeling or motive; safety lines missing their condition (play dead only once a surprised grizzly has knocked you down; bear spray only on a charging or attacking bear); numbers without their unit or year. 21 fixes in five Shorts, about 25 in two TikTok cuts, none of them a wrong safety line.
