## 3. What the advice says
- **One repeatable format drives most growth** (Galloway, opinion with case studies): a test that gets 2x your
  average twice is a winning format; "70%+ of growth comes from 1 or 2 ideas built into formats"; "saturating the
  audience" is a myth. Schedule he recommends: 1 long-form a week + 3+ Shorts a week.
- **Packaging:** top creators spend ~30% of their time on idea + title + thumbnail; small creators ~5% (Galloway, claim).
- **Long-form converts better** (2023 study, 5,400 Shorts / 33 channels): 22.7 vs 16.9 subs per 10K views;
  50-60 s Shorts averaged the most views. Old data; directional only.
- **Small channels are being rewarded in 2026** (Metricool, 799,718 videos, data): 2K-10K tier +210% Shorts views
  year on year, 1M+ tier -74%. Per-post views barely change from 2-4/week to 7+/week: volume does not buy reach.
  Channels posting ~5 Shorts/month had ~2x the long-form views of channels with none (correlation).
- **First test is viewed vs swiped away.** The retention cut-offs blogs quote (50%, 65%, 70%) are unsourced and
  contradict each other. Don't build on them.
- **Monetization risk:** the July 2025 inauthentic-content policy can block YPP for mass-produced uploads with the
  same voiceover every time (Metricool; check YouTube's own wording before relying on it).

## 4. Recommendation (for the Mayor to decide)
1. "Things Living In Your House" = the main Shorts series. Numbered, 50-60 s, one drawn style, "#N tomorrow" end card.
   3-5 a week.
2. Pause "Meatball Investigates" Shorts (1-431 views over 5 tries). Occasional test only.
3. One long-form a week in the same format: "Every Creature Living In Your House, Explained" (8-15 min),
   Paint-Explainer title shape. Link each Short to it.
4. Five titles per video before production.
5. Keep/kill rule: 2x average twice = make a Part 2; under that after ~5 tries = drop.
6. Weekly metric: subscribers per 1,000 views.
