## The three reasons it loops
### 1. The queue is 4 weeks long and 87% random facts
- `posted.csv` holds **112 scheduled posts** from 10/30 to 11/26.
- Of those, **97 are one-off facts** ("This Bat Drinks So Much Blood...", "This Tortoise Can Go a Whole Year..."), **13 are House**, **1 is Survive** and **1 is Spider**.
- The new drawn series episodes are mostly **not queued at all**. Is This Spider Venomous? #1 is queued for **11/26**, at the very back.
- So every rule change (series only, drawn only, loop endings) reaches viewers **a month late**, and the next 4 weeks of uploads are the format the data says to stop. The plan changes; the channel doesn't.

### 2. Every Short lands on the same plateau
- Latest views (10/28): the top 12 Shorts all sit at **1,000-2,000**, and new House episodes land at 900-1,300.
- Nothing breaks out, so YouTube tests each Short with a small audience and stops.
- **Stayed-to-watch 39.5%** is the brake: YouTube expands a Short only when most of the first audience keeps watching. Series openers (58.6%) clear it; True/False and generic fact openers (20-22%) kill it.

### 3. Viewers never come back
- **90 days: 115,000 new viewers vs 2,847 returning (2.5%).** One-off facts give no reason to watch the next one.
- Only Would Meatball Survive? (Honey Badger: 181 returning) and the House show build any memory.
- Subs: 959 → 967 in a day, at 28,900 views in 28 days (~0.3 subs per 1,000 views).

## The rule that stops the loop
**A decision isn't made until it's in the queue.** If a new rule (style, show, ending) doesn't change what posts in the next 7 days, it didn't happen. The Mayor checks this every evening:
- What % of the next 7 days of YouTube posts are House / Survive / Spider? Target 100%.
- Is anything queued more than 7 days out? Target: no.
