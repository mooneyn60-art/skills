# Meatball Myths: monetization plan

The shortest honest path to the YouTube Partner Program (YPP), from research on 10/27. Built for the Meatball Town agent.

Last Updated: 2026-10-27
Status: active
Audience: Nolan, Meatball Town (Mayor, Business, Showrunner, Studio)

## Overview
Nolan: "I need to scale this thing, the plan is all profits get invested... I need to get monetized on YouTube." He also reports that, on his channel, Shorts do best **under 20 s with a loop**. This plan follows that.

## The rules today (verified)
- **YouTube's YPP page** ([support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851)):
  - Join with **1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months**, OR **1,000 subscribers plus 10M qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days**.
  - Shorts-feed views do NOT count toward watch hours.
  - Views and hours from ad campaigns (paid promotion) count toward neither path.
- **A change is coming:**
  - YouTube's page says the YPP updates start **February 1, 2027**, and creators must accept the new terms by January 31, 2027.
  - MediaPost ([article](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/417147/youtube-doubles-eligibility-requirements-for-creat.html)) reports the new entry bar roughly **doubles: 8,000 watch hours or 20M Shorts views**, starting "soon". It also reports that from Feb 1, 2027, channels must keep up 10M Shorts views per 90 days to keep Shorts revenue.
  - **So the goal is to qualify and apply under today's rules, before February 2027.**
- **The review is a real gate** ([TechCrunch, 7/20/2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)). Three kinds of "inauthentic content" can't be monetized:
  1. **Generic, repetitive or template-based content**, "that AI, CGI, or templates can make easily, with little variation". YouTube's description: "very generic... no narrative arc... don't show your creativity."
  2. **Distressing content to drive views**, e.g. an animal in distress then rescued.
  3. **AI personas on sensitive topics.**

  A channel with too much of any of these can't monetize. **This is the biggest hidden risk for us:** templated 20 s photo Shorts with an AI voice, posted several times a day, look like category 1. Hitting the numbers with that library could still fail the review.

## Which path
**Path A, 10M Shorts views in 90 days:**
- Needs ~110K views a day, about 50x today.
- Only possible with several breakouts (small animated channels do hit 1-10M on a single Short: Nostril Animation 4M; animal-transformation Shorts 2-9M).
- Keep swinging for it, but don't bet the plan on it.

**Path B, 4,000 watch hours, using compilations (the main path):**
- Small channels in this niche earn watch hours with **long compilations**:
  - "25 of America's Strangest Mysteries", 50 min: 236K views on a 2.6K-sub channel (692x).
  - "Morbid Facts... Best of August", 2 h 7 min: 128K on 7.1K subs.
  - A 5-hour Sam O'Nella compilation: 115K.
  - "Toon Kart animation compilation #10": 836K.
- **Math:**
  - A 40-60 min compilation at ~6-8 min average view = **~8-10 views per watch hour**.
  - **4,000 hours ≈ 32,000-40,000 compilation views in total.**
  - Example: 16 compilations at 2,500 views each, or one breakout plus a handful.
  - Compare 11-minute single episodes at ~3 min average: you'd need ~80,000 views.
- **Content source:** our own Shorts, re-cut by theme ("Every Animal That Could Kill Meatball: The Complete Collection"). That is our own original material, NOT reused content from others. But to pass the inauthentic-content review, each compilation must add real value:
  - a new cold open and chapter intros where Meatball links segments
  - running gags and callbacks
  - a ranking or countdown spine (the brainbites structure: checkpoints, a mid-point flip, a final leaderboard)
  - chapters in the description

  Never a raw stitch of Shorts with no new narration.

## The plan (in order)
1. **Fix the review risk first, alongside step 2.** Move the house look to the NICHE_PLAYBOOK spec:
   - one drawn world
   - Meatball acting inside scenes
   - animals with faces
   - a narrative arc in every Short (setup → escalation → twist)

   This is also what makes Shorts break out. It is the single change that serves both the views and the review.
2. **Shorts stay ≤20 s with a seamless loop on YouTube** (Nolan's own data: Daddy Longlegs 20 s at ~2,000 vs the 43-88 s Shorts at 281-410).
   - 2026 outliers back it: animal-transformation Shorts at 12-31 s with 2-9M views.
   - TikTok gets 60-90 s versions (Creator Rewards pays only 60 s+).
   - One drawn asset set feeds both.
3. **Compilation engine, starting this week:**
   - Publish **1 compilation a week, 30-60 min**, built from the best-performing series ("Would Meatball Survive?: Every Episode", "Every Animal That Could Kill You, Explained").
   - Pin it as the channel trailer and featured video.
   - Set each Short's **Related video** to the newest compilation (Nolan, in YouTube Studio; it's the only link from a Short into long-form).
   - Thumbnail and title promise a "complete collection" or a "ranked" list.
   - Measure watch hours added per compilation each week.
4. **Stop the single 11-min daily episodes.** They earn almost nothing. Put that render time into compilations and better Shorts.
5. **Subscriber engine:**
   - Numbered series titles ("#14").
   - A spoken reason at the loop point ("there are 40 more of these").
   - Community posts.

   With 957 subs, a series that converts at 0.5-1 sub per 1,000 views crosses 1,000 within a week of a decent Short.
6. **Track the two numbers that decide YPP:**
   - watch hours (12-month rolling)
   - Shorts views (90-day)

   Both are in Studio's Earn tab; the Accountant posts them daily. Every decision gets judged on "does this add watch hours or breakout odds?"
7. **Apply the day both are met,** before the new rules land. Before applying, run an **inauthentic-content self-audit** on the last 30 uploads:
   - Is each one different in story?
   - Does each have an arc?
   - Is Meatball doing something creative?

   Pause any series that is a pure template.

## Where the reinvested money should go (highest return first)
1. **The look:** a professional style sheet plus an asset library for Meatball (poses, expressions, mouth shapes) and the recurring animals, by a human illustrator or animator. One-time spend; every video uses it. It's also the best defence in the review.
2. **Voice:** a premium, consistent narrator voice (a licensed TTS voice or a voice actor) with real comic timing. Voice is half the product on Shorts.
3. **Sound effects and music licences:** an SFX on every action is a retention lever.
4. **NOT on YouTube ads or promoted views:** they count toward neither monetization path. TikTok Promote only for testing which hooks travel, never to "buy" the threshold.

## Honest odds
- **YPP by January 31, 2027 via watch hours:** possible but not likely without at least one compilation getting suggested traffic. TARS estimate **~25-30%**.
- **Via 10M Shorts views:** ~10-15%.
- **Either one:** ~35%.

The biggest swing factor is the new look. If the illustrated test wins, raise these. If YouTube's entry bar doubles before we qualify, the compilation library still keeps earning hours toward 8,000, so nothing is wasted.
