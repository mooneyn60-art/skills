## Bottom line
1. **Our current Shorts match YouTube's own example of what can't be monetized.** support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392 lists "**Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative**". TARS's frame-by-frame review describes our Shorts as "a still stock photo with a slow zoom, a small clip-art stick figure". That is an image slideshow on a template, posted 4-7 times a day.
2. **AI voice is not the problem, and neither is a cartoon host or compilations of our own work.** The problem is a reviewer opening the channel and seeing **"little variation from video to video"** (the July 2026 clarification). Tubefilter quotes a violation as "the same situation over and over again with the same outcome". That fits "Would Meatball Survive?" if every episode ends the same way.
3. **The timeline is tighter than the plan assumes.**
   - A decision takes "typically about 1 month".
   - A first rejection means a **30-day** wait to re-apply.
   - YouTube has said nothing about applications pending on Feb 1, 2027.
   - To be accepted under the 4,000 h rules, **apply by about Dec 15 with both bars met, and pass first time.** One rejection almost certainly pushes us onto the 8,000 h rules.
4. **My estimate of rejection on the first review:**
   - **as-is (photo slideshows at volume): 60-70%**
   - **after the fixes below: 20-30%**

   The subagent's estimates were 55-70% and 20-35%. The evidence base is news-level, not a dataset.

## Policy text (verified on YouTube's pages)
- **Not monetizable:**
  - "Similar or repetitive content with low educational value, commentary, narratives, or minimal variation across videos"
  - "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value"
  - "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production"
- **Allowed:**
  - "Same intro and outro... but the bulk of your content is different"
  - a series where "each video has a distinct storyline, focus, or concept"
  - AI "to generate a unique background visual", provided the final product shows "your creative vision"
- **Reused content:** content copied from elsewhere "without substantive modifications" is not monetizable, "even if you have permission". The Shorts monetization page also excludes "compilations with no original content added".
- **July 2026 clarification:** three buckets can't be monetized:
  - generic or template content
  - content "designed to be distressing", e.g. an animal in distress, then rescued
  - AI personas on sensitive topics (health, legal, financial, political)

  A channel with "too much" of any of them can't monetize. "Too much" isn't defined.
- **Reviewers look at:**
  - main theme
  - most-viewed videos
  - newest videos
  - videos with the biggest share of watch time
  - metadata
  - About section
- **AI disclosure label:** required only for *realistic* synthetic content. Fully animated cartoons are exempt; photoreal AI animals or fake "archival" footage are not.

## Channel-specific red flags (blunt)
1. **Photo slideshow look at 4-7 Shorts a day** matches the exact policy wording. Fix: the drawn-world pipeline (item 05), and cut to 1-2 Shorts a day.
2. **About 140 queued Shorts in one template.** The reviewer samples the *newest* uploads. If the queue is still draining slideshows in December, those are what they see.
   - **Stop scheduling slideshow Shorts after about Nov 25.**
   - **Make the weakest near-duplicates private 30 days before applying.**
3. **Conspiracy and case files.**
   - Not a listed bucket, but "AI personas on sensitive topics" covers *political* topics.
   - The Factdesk red list (no elections or medical claims) and on-screen PROVEN/BUSTED/UNSOLVED verdicts are right; keep them.
   - **One fabricated "real photo" claim would be the True Crime Case Files pattern.** TARS already caught one near-miss: the CIA painting presented as a photo.
4. **Compilations as the main source of watch time.** Reviewers look at the videos holding the most watch time. Every compilation needs a new cold open, Meatball link lines, a ranking or theme, and chapters. Never stitch raw.
5. **"Would Meatball Survive?" with the same outcome every time** risks the "same situation, same outcome" language. Vary the endings: sometimes he wins, sometimes the animal is the hero.

## Pre-application self-audit (run on Dec 1 and again the day before applying; each item is yes/no)
1. Is each of the 10 newest uploads a distinct fact or story, not a word-swap on one template?
2. Do the 10 most-viewed videos show Meatball doing something different each time, with varied outcomes?
3. Do the 20 newest Shorts look visibly varied (scenes, camera, layouts), with **zero** still-photo slideshows?
4. Has volume been ≤2 Shorts a day for the last 3 weeks, with weak near-duplicates set to private ≥30 days before applying?
5. Does every script have a source line, with sources in the description?
6. Is there **zero** invented "true" history, fake quote, or painting passed off as a photo?
7. Is there no animal in distress or peril for shock value?
8. Is every realistic AI visual labelled as altered/synthetic, or removed?
9. Does each compilation add a new cold open, link narration, ranking or theme, and chapters?
10. Is no video built from other creators' clips, Shorts or reposted stock footage?
11. Are there at least 5 original long-forms (not compilations) public on the channel?
12. Do the videos with the most watch time look the most original?
13. Does the About section name the human creator, describe the process (human research and writing, AI-assisted voice and animation), and present Meatball as an original character? (Today's About already says "Animated with AI tools; the research and fact-checking are ours". Good; add the creator's name.)
14. Are titles and thumbnails accurate, not template-identical, and not clickbait that misleads?
15. Is an unlisted appeal video (<5 min) ready, showing script drafts, research, the character design and editing?
16. Is every music track, SFX, font and image licensed? Is the CC BY-SA attribution done?
17. Is the TTS voice under a commercial licence, and not a clone of a real person?
18. Are there zero Community Guidelines strikes, and is the made-for-kids setting correct?
19. Are there 1,000 subs and 4,000 public long-form hours, with the application filed by about **Dec 15**?
20. Is there one human touch, e.g. Nolan's voice or a creator note on the channel page or in one video?
