# 01. Watch-hours engine: long compilations

Meatball Research Lab, 2026-10-27. Data: `data/compilations.csv`.
vidIQ spent on this item: **25 credits** (2 long-form outlier searches = 10, 1 metadata batch = 5, 2 keyword searches = 10).

## Bottom line
1. **Long compilations in this niche do break out on tiny channels**, and the winners share one shape: a number in the title, a "you weren't told" or "sounds fake" promise, 50 min to 3.5 h, and a chapter per item.
2. **The base rate is brutal.** Each winner's outlier score implies its channel's *typical* compilation gets about **100-800 views**:
   - Atlas To Ashes: 236K ÷ 692x ≈ 340
   - The History Dude: 247K ÷ 2,560x ≈ 100
   - Dumb History: 138K ÷ 335x ≈ 410
   - Slumber Science: 146K ÷ 247x ≈ 590

   At 100-800 views and 6-10 min average view, one typical compilation earns about **10-130 hours**. 4,000 h needs either one breakout or 30+ ordinary uploads. We have 13 weeks.
3. **"Facts to fall asleep to" is the highest-hours format.**
   - Search demand: "facts to fall asleep to" **64K/month** (comp 56); "history to fall asleep" **61K/month** (comp **36**, the lowest competition in this research).
   - These run 2-3.5 h, and sleep viewers leave them playing, so hours per view are several times a 45-min list.
   - A 2.7K-sub channel (Slumber Science) got **146K views** on a 2.5 h video in 6 weeks (247x its norm).
4. **The rules changed under us.** YouTube confirmed on 2026-08-10 that from **Feb 1, 2027** new applicants need **8,000 hours** (or 20M Shorts views in 90 days). The subscriber-conversion agent traced this to blog.youtube and support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009.
   - It is no longer "roughly doubles, reportedly".
   - YouTube has published nothing on applications still pending on Feb 1. **Treat Jan 31 as the day the application must be submitted with both bars met, not the day we start applying.**

### What the winners share
- **Title:**
  - a number (25, 100, 111)
  - a "secret / they didn't teach you / sounds fake / you didn't ask for" frame
  - and either a sleep promise ("to Fall Asleep To") or a "Best of [month]" / "Part N" frame
- **Length:** 50 min to 3.5 h. The sleep ones run 2-3.5 h.
- **Chapters:** Atlas To Ashes puts a timestamp on every item, about every 2 min (25 chapters in 50 min). The History Dude uses 7 broad chapters. Both rank.
- **Traffic (inferred; vidIQ can't show other channels' sources):**
  - The 1.1K-sub History Dude is still doing **51 views/hour four months after upload**, and Atlas To Ashes did **636/h at one month**. Rates like that on near-zero-sub channels are suggested/browse and sleep-search long tails, not subscriber traffic.
  - Engagement is low (0.5-1.6% likes), typical of background viewing.
- **Reused-content warning:** Morbid Chronicles and the Sam O'Nella upload are compilations of *other people's* videos. They get views, but they are exactly what the reused-content rule excludes from YPP. **Our compilations must be 100% our own material plus new narration.**

## The math for 4,000 h (and 8,000 h after Feb 1)
`hours = views × average view duration (AVD)`

| Format | Realistic AVD for us | Views for 4,000 h | Views for 8,000 h |
|---|---|---|---|
| 45-60 min ranked compilation | 4-8 min | 30,000-60,000 | 60,000-120,000 |
| 2-3 h "to fall asleep to" | 15-30 min | 8,000-16,000 | 16,000-32,000 |
| current 5-7 min long-forms | ~0.5 min (actual 28 s today) | 480,000 | 960,000 |

- The AVD figures are my assumptions. Our only measured long-form AVD is **28 s**, and long-forms get 4-101 views.
- The sleep AVD is the least certain figure in this file. It comes from format logic (people fall asleep with it playing), not measured data.
- **Watch-time math alone makes the current 5-7 min long-forms close to worthless for YPP.**

## Spec: Meatball's first 3 compilations
All three are 16:9, built only from the channel's own scripts, renders and art. Each has:
- a new 20-30 s cold open with Meatball in the scene
- a new Meatball link line between items, so it is not a raw stitch
- a chapter per item in the description
- Related video on every Short pointing at the newest one
- a pinned comment listing the chapters
- **no** mid-video subscribe asks before minute 3

### C1. "Weird Animal Facts to Fall Asleep To (2 Hours, No Jump Scares)"
- **Why:** the highest hours per view, plus search demand: 64K/month "facts to fall asleep to" and 31K "weird animal facts".
- **Structure:**
  - 60-80 facts from the animal pillar (cassowary, box jellyfish, stonefish, ghost crab, daddy longlegs and the rest), re-narrated slow: about 120 wpm, calm and low.
  - Visuals change every 8-15 s (slow pans on drawn scenes). No loud SFX, no music spikes. Meatball appears small and sleepy in a corner, yawning at chapter breaks.
- **Chapters:** one every 10 min by habitat (Your House, The Garden, The Beach, The Deep Sea, The Jungle...).
- **Length:** 2:00-2:30. **Thumbnail:** Meatball asleep in a hammock under a moon, one strange glowing animal, text "FALL ASLEEP TO THIS".
- **Expected views (90 days):** median 300-1,500. P(≥10K) **15%**.
- **Expected hours:** at 20 min AVD, a 1,500-view result gives 500 h, and 10K views gives 3,300 h.

### C2. "25 Secret Government Projects That Sound Fake (But Were Real)"
- **Why:** it reuses the case-file research already done (A119, Northwoods, Azorian, Iceworm, Popeye, bat bombs, Pig War...). It is the Atlas To Ashes template: 25 items, 50 min, a chapter every 2 min, number plus "sound fake".
- **Structure:**
  - Countdown from 25 to 1. Each item gets a 90-120 s story beat: scene, rewind, twist, verdict stamp.
  - Checkpoint recap at #13 ("the list flips: the next 12 actually happened to civilians").
  - Final board.
  - Factdesk red list applies; no living private people.
- **Length:** 45-55 min. **Thumbnail:** Meatball holding a CLASSIFIED folder with one absurd drawn image (a nuke and the Moon), text "THEY WERE REAL".
- **Expected:** median 200-1,000 views. P(≥10K) **10%**. Hours at 6 min AVD: 1K views ≈ 100 h.

### C3. "History Facts to Fall Asleep To: 100 Weird True Stories (3 Hours)"
- **Why:** "history to fall asleep" is **61K/month at competition 36**, the softest big keyword found. It reuses C2's cases plus the weird-history Shorts.
- **Structure:** same sleep rules as C1, 100 items, chapters by century.
- **Expected:** median 300-1,500. P(≥10K) **12%**.

**Cadence:** C1 by Nov 7, C2 by Nov 14, C3 by Nov 21, then one per week, alternating sleep and ranked, through mid-January. That makes about 12 compilations.

| Event | Probability |

These are below TARS's 25-30% because of the base rate: typical compilations on the very channels that produced the breakouts get 100-800 views.

## Recommendations
1. **Build C1 (sleep format) first, this week.**
   - Probability it out-earns the next 5 regular long-forms combined in hours within 30 days: **75%**.
2. **Stop the Tue/Sat 5-7 min long-forms now and move that render time to compilations.**
   - Probability this costs us any YPP progress: **<10%**. They earn about 0 hours: 4-101 views at 28 s AVD.
3. **Nolan sets Related video on every Short to the newest compilation** (Studio only).
   - Expected: about 1% click-through (from the sub-conversion agent: YouTube publishes no CTR; third-party anecdotes run from under 1% to 5%).
   - At 6K Shorts views/day that is about 60 clicks/day, roughly 5-10 h/day of watch time. Over 90 days: **450-900 h**. That is a real chunk, not a solution.
   - Probability it adds ≥300 h by Jan 31: **55%**.
4. **Every compilation uses only our own material plus new narration and a Meatball cold open.**
   - Probability a raw stitch (no new narration) gets flagged as reused or inauthentic at review: **~50%**.
   - With the added layer: **~15%** (see item 04).
