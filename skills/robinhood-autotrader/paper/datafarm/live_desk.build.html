How to read this desk

Honest summary from ~650 tests: no line on this chart predicts direction by itself. What does hold up is context: the time of day, the gap and its fill odds, the expected move, and news days. Use the lines as a map for where to put stops and targets, and to stay on the right side of the trend.

Before you buy, check:
  1. Is it after 10:30?
  2. Trend row agrees with your direction (above VWAP and EMA 9 > 21 for calls)?
  3. Where's your stop on the chart (a level, VWAP or a trend line), and is the target at least 2× the risk?
  4. Does the move you need fit inside the expected move left?
  5. Inside your rules: trips, daily limit, max premium?
VWAP ±1σ ±2σ · The average price paid today, weighted by volume, with bands one and two standard deviations away.
Watch
Is price above or below it? How far, in σ? Does price keep coming back to it?
How to use it
The day's fair price. Above = buyers in control, below = sellers. Moves past ±2σ are stretched and often snap back toward VWAP. A clean place to put a stop on the other side.
TARS tested
Crossing VWAP did not predict direction (133 days). Useful as a map, not a trigger.
EMA 9 / 21 · Two fast moving averages of the close.
Watch
Is 9 above 21 (up) or below (down)? Are they flat and tangled (chop)?
How to use it
Trend filter: only take calls when 9 > 21 and price is above VWAP; the desk's Trend row does this for you.
TARS tested
EMA crosses alone: no edge on 20 years of SPY. Fine as a filter.
Trend lines (auto) · A line through the last two clear swing highs (resistance) and swing lows (support), extended to now. Dashed with ✗ = price has closed through it.
Watch
Price approaching a line, and whether it breaks with heavy volume or rejects.
How to use it
Rising support + price above VWAP = uptrend intact. A break of support with volume = trend may be over; tighten stops. Lines redraw as new swings form.
TARS tested
Not tested as a signal. Treat as a picture of the trend, nothing more.
PDH / PDL / OR · Yesterday's high, low and close, and the first 15 minutes' high and low (opening range).
Watch
Where price sits against them, and whether a break holds or snaps back.
How to use it
These are the levels everyone sees, so stops pile up there. Good places for targets and stops.
TARS tested
Opening-range breaks did not predict direction out of sample.
Day high/low + 10:30 · Today's high and low so far (HOD/LOD), and a line at 10:30.
Watch
Before 10:30: just watch. Your record: lost 5 of 5 days trading before 10:30.
How to use it
Most of the day's range forms early: ~42% by 10:00, ~77% by 11:30. New highs late in the day are rarer.
TARS tested
Holds up: the shape-of-day numbers come from 20 years of SPY.
Volume · Shares traded per candle along the bottom; bright bars = more than 1.5× the recent average.
Watch
Breakouts on heavy volume vs. light volume. Volume dries up at lunch.
How to use it
A move with heavy volume is more likely to be real participation; a move on thin volume is easier to reverse.
TARS tested
Not tested as a signal here.
Bollinger 20/2 · A 20-candle average with bands two standard deviations away. Width shows how quiet or wild it is.
Watch
Squeezes (bands narrow) and price riding a band.
How to use it
Narrow bands = quiet, a bigger move often follows (direction unknown). Riding the upper band = strong trend, not automatically 'overbought'.
TARS tested
Bollinger squeezes: no direction edge on 20 years.
Pivots · Floor-trader pivot levels from yesterday's high, low, close: P, R1/R2 above, S1/S2 below.
Watch
Reactions at P and R1/S1.
How to use it
Widely watched, so they can act like PDH/PDL: places for targets and stops.
TARS tested
Like Fibonacci, expect them to work about as well as any other level.
Liquidity pools · Equal highs or lows where stops tend to sit. 'Swept' = price ran through and closed back inside. The ▲/▼ tag next to the price says which pool gets hit first, and the odds.
Watch
The ▲/▼ tag and the "Next pool to get hit" row. Above 70% = the nearer pool is a strong favorite; 50-60% = coin flip.
How to use it
The odds come from distance: the closer pool gets hit first more often, in proportion (pool 0.30 above and 0.70 below → ~70% up first). Use the favored pool as a realistic first target, and the other one as where your idea is wrong. It says where price is likely to travel next, not how far the day goes after.
TARS tested
Which pool gets hit first: calibrated on 30 days (1,668 checks): called 10/30/50/71/90% up, actual 9/27/52/69/88%. Adding trend, MACD, VWAP side or RSI did not improve it. Whether a break keeps going: no edge.
Structure BOS/CHoCH · What paid indicators like "Godmode" sell: a dashed line where price closed through the last confirmed swing. CHoCH = first break against the trend, BOS = a break with it.
Watch
Whether the last break was up or down, and where it happened. It's a map of who's in control, nothing more.
How to use it
Use it for context and to place stops beyond the swing that broke. Don't buy just because a CHoCH or BOS printed.
TARS tested
Rebuilt and run on 164 days of SPY 5-min bars (10:30-14:30, stop at structure, 2R target, 60 min, after costs). BOS: −0.05 pts/trade, 47% wins (n=241, out-of-sample). CHoCH: +0.01, 48% (n=257). Sweep then CHoCH (the headline "reversal" signal): +0.50 and 60% wins in one sample (n=20) but −0.21 and 37% in the other (n=81). It looks great on hand-picked charts and loses on all of them. No edge.

Expected move · The 1σ move left today, from the options market's implied volatility.
Watch
Price near the edge of the band.
How to use it
SPY stays inside ±1σ about 65% of days, and real moves run ~60% of what options price. Don't buy options needing a move past the band.
TARS tested
