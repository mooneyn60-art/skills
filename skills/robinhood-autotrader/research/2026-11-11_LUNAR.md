## The claim and mechanism (written before the test)

Dichev & Janes (2003, J. Investing) and Yuan, Zheng & Zhu (2006, J. Empirical
Finance, 48 countries) report returns several percentage points a year higher
in the 15 days around new moons than around full moons. The mechanism offered
is mood: sleep and mood are said to dip around the full moon, making
investors more pessimistic. The mechanism is weak (the sleep evidence is
contested), and with hundreds of calendar anomalies tested worldwide, some
look strong by chance. That's why it's a good test of whether our hurdles
kill nonsense.

## Test (fixed before running)

- Moon phase from the mean synodic month (29.530588853 days), anchored to the
  new moon of 2000-01-06 18:14 UTC. Mean-phase error is under about a day,
  well inside a 15-day window.
- New-moon window: the 15 calendar days centred on each new moon (phase age
  0-7.4 and 22.1-29.5 days). Full-moon window: the other 15.
- Series: SPY close-to-close (paper/history/daily_ohlcv.json, 2006-2026) and
  the equal-weight average of the 9 non-megacap names.
- Statistic: mean daily return new-window minus full-window, Welch t.
  Annualised gap reported for comparison with the papers.
- Hurdles: split halves; 9 windows (>= 5 of 9 positive); non-megacap basket.

## PREDICTION

- SPY new-minus-full gap between -0.03 and +0.03pp a day (about +/-7.5pp a
  year), |t| < 1.5.
- Sign flips between halves; walk-forward 3-6 of 9.
- Non-megacap basket: same, |t| < 1.5.
- Dead. If it isn't dead, the first suspect is a data or phase error, not
  the moon.

## Reading it

- No lunar effect. The first-half SPY gap (+7.7pp a year) is the kind of
  number the papers report, and it flips to -3.5pp in the second half.
- One thing worth keeping: SPY was "ahead in 5 of 9 windows", which on
  its own passes the walk-forward hurdle. A known-nonsense anomaly can
  clear 5/9 by luck. It was the t-stat and the sign flip between halves that
  killed it. The walk-forward count is a necessary hurdle, never a
  sufficient one.
