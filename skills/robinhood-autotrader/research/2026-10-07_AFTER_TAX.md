# Taxes: Does TARS Still Beat Buy-and-Hold SPY After Tax?

One-line: RESEARCH_AGENDA item 14. TARS realises gains every few weeks, taxed
as ordinary income; holding SPY defers tax until a single sale at the
long-term rate. How much of TARS's pre-tax edge survives?

Last Updated: 2026-10-07
Status: DONE. Prediction committed first (3e7730f); mostly WRONG: taxes hurt TARS less than predicted.
Audience: Nolan, TARS sessions

## Model (fixed before running)

- TARS-1 as it stands (paper/engine.py; same config as the R15 integration
  test), 14 names, 2006-2026, price returns. SPY buy-and-hold over the same
  days, price only (no dividends on either side, so the comparison is
  like-for-like).
- Engine trades now carry their exit day, so realised gains can be assigned
  to calendar years. Holding > 252 trading days = long-term.
- Each year: net short-term and long-term gains separately; losses offset
  gains, and a net loss carries forward (the $3,000-a-year ordinary-income
  offset is ignored). Tax is paid at year-end out of the account, which
  shrinks the base that compounds afterwards.
- SPY: no tax until one sale on the last day, at the long-term rate. Also
  reported: SPY never sold (no tax at all).
- Nolan's bracket is not recorded. Four federal scenarios (ordinary /
  long-term): 12%/0%, 22%/15%, 24%/15%, 32%/15%. No state tax. Not tax advice
  (reference/TAX_TREATMENT.md).

## PREDICTION

- Pre-tax: TARS ahead of SPY by ~2.5-3pp a year (as earlier tests found).
- Under 15% of TARS's realised gains are long-term.
- After tax at 22%/15%: TARS's lead shrinks to 1pp or less; at 32%/15% SPY
  is ahead after tax; at 12%/0% TARS stays ahead by ~1.5-2pp.
- Expected verdict: at a typical bracket, taxes erase most of TARS's
  backtested edge, which item 8 already found statistically weak.

## Answer first

- TAXES ARE NOT WHAT DECIDES THIS. In the backtest, TARS-1's tax drag is
  0.45-1.83pp a year depending on bracket, and it stays ahead of
  buy-and-hold SPY after tax in every scenario (+1.5 to +2.2pp a year).
- WHY THE DRAG IS SMALL: only 22 of 444 trades were held over a year, but
  those 22 produced most of the profit (net realised long-term gains 4.3x
  starting equity against 1.2x short-term). Letting winners run under the
  20% trail turns the big winners into long-term gains. The many short
  trades roughly wash.
- BUT THE WHOLE LEAD IS THE MEGACAPS AGAIN. Without AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, GOOGL
  and AMZN (supplementary run, not pre-registered), TARS-1 makes 4.38% a
  year before tax against SPY's 9.03%, and about 3.9% after. Taxes barely
  matter there because the short-term trades net to a LOSS. Survivorship
  (item 5) and the statistical weakness of the edge (item 8: t=0.50 vs SPY)
  still apply to the headline numbers.
- THE LIVE ACCOUNT IS NOT THE BACKTEST. Every live trade so far is
  short-term, and the account's recent results are dominated by owner 0DTE
  options (NAV index -8.55% on 2026-10-07). Short-term LOSSES do offset
  other income for tax (up to $3,000 a year against ordinary income,
  carried forward beyond that), which is a small consolation, not a strategy.

## For TARS (interpretation)

- No rule change follows. The 20% trail's main virtue here is a side
  effect: it holds big winners past a year, which is tax-efficient.
  Tightening it would cost tax as well as return.
- For Nolan's own trading: short-dated options are 100% short-term, taxed
  as ordinary income when they win. The tax maths only favours holding
  winners a long time, the opposite of 0DTE.
