# Oh Meatball — YouTube Algorithm Playbook (researched 2026-10-19)

Channel: Oh Meatball (UCw98RQD81t_339LX5EJqcrg), ~940 subs, recent Shorts 0-30 views.
Format: realistic AI "caught on camera" animal clips (doorbell/security/dashcam look),
8-15 s, plot-twist titles, AI disclosure ON. Plus weekly compilations and long-form.
Posting: Metricool, 10 AM / 4 PM ET.

Confidence tags: **[OFFICIAL]** = YouTube Help / YouTube exec on record.
**[CREDIBLE]** = reputable press or large-sample study. **[UNVERIFIED]** = creator/vendor
blog folklore; use as a hypothesis, not a fact.

## 0. The five rules that matter most

1. **The first second decides everything.** Shorts live or die on "Viewed vs. Swiped
   away" in the feed. No intro, no establishing shot: the animal is already moving at
   frame 1. [OFFICIAL metric; benchmark UNVERIFIED]
2. **Never make "animal in distress" content.** Since 16 Jul 2026, YPP explicitly
   demonetizes channels dedicated to distressing / emotionally manipulative content,
   with "animal in distress then rescued" as YouTube's named example, *AI or not*.
   Predator-vs-pet, injured animals, near-misses on roads = off-limits. Funny, cheeky,
   wholesome twists only. [OFFICIAL via TechCrunch / Matt Halprin]
3. **Vary every clip.** "Inauthentic content" (ex-"repetitious content") kills
   monetization for channels whose videos are template-made with the same scenario and
   outcome. Same camera frame is fine; same story beat every time is not. [OFFICIAL]
4. **Judge by batches of 10+, not single uploads.** New-channel Shorts medians stay flat
   across the first 10 uploads; growth comes from the occasional outlier. Volume buys
   lottery tickets. [CREDIBLE, vidIQ 2026 study]
5. **Keep the AI disclosure ON for realistic clips.** It is required for realistic
   depictions of events that didn't happen, and YouTube says it does not reduce reach or
   monetization eligibility. Not disclosing risks a forced label, removal, or YPP
   suspension. [OFFICIAL]

## 1. Shorts feed ranking (how it works now)

### What we know
- **Views counting changed 31 Mar 2025**: every start or replay counts as a "view"; no
  minimum watch time. The old metric is now **Engaged views** (Analytics → Advanced
  mode). YPP eligibility and Shorts revenue use **engaged views**, not views.
  [OFFICIAL] → Track engaged views; raw views are inflated by loops.
- **Feed = involuntary viewing.** Most Shorts views come from the feed rather than a
  viewer choosing the video, so YouTube tests each Short on viewers and reads their
  reaction (Todd Sherman, Shorts product lead, on Creator Insider, via vidIQ). [CREDIBLE]
- **Core signal: Viewed vs. Swiped away** (Studio → Analytics → Content → Shorts →
  "How many chose to view"). YouTube does not publish the seconds cutoff. [OFFICIAL]
- **Officially named recommendation inputs**: viewing behavior, likes, dislikes,
  subscriptions, explicit feedback incl. satisfaction surveys, and channel
  reputation/quality. [OFFICIAL, How YouTube Works] No published weights.
- **Retention**: avg % viewed and replays/loops matter (a loop is both a view and a
  completion). Exact weighting unknown. [CREDIBLE consensus]
- **Shares and comments** are commonly cited as strong signals; not officially weighted.
  [UNVERIFIED but universal advice]

### Seed testing and why videos stall at 0-50 views
- Model widely used by creators: each Short goes to a small **seed audience**, then
  expands if retention/swipe metrics beat comparable Shorts ("explore then exploit").
  Claimed seed size ~1,500-2,500 viewers; claimed test window 30-60 min. [UNVERIFIED]
- **A Short stuck at 0-30 views usually means it never got (or failed) the first test.**
  Likely causes, most to least likely for us:
  1. YouTube can't place the video: it doesn't know who it's for because the channel
     has no recent history in this niche (see channel-history section).
  2. Weak first second → high swipe-away in the tiny test → distribution stops.
  3. Classifier flagged it as low-quality/mass-produced AI (more scrutiny since the
     Jan 2026 "AI slop" crackdown). [CREDIBLE that crackdown exists; mechanism UNVERIFIED]
  4. Technical: region restrictions, "made for kids" mis-set, scheduler-posted Shorts
     not recognized as Shorts (must be vertical/square, <=3 min). Check each.
- Shorts can **revive weeks later** when YouTube finds a matching audience. Don't
  delete underperformers (deleting has no known benefit and loses that chance).
  [UNVERIFIED but widely observed]

### Channel history / audience mismatch (old cat/story audience)
- Rene Ritchie (YouTube Creator Liaison) has repeatedly said performance is judged per
  video/topic rather than per channel, so one weak video doesn't poison the next.
  Exact quotes circulating online are unverified. [UNVERIFIED wording]
- In practice: Shorts are not shown to subscribers first; feed distribution is driven by
  interest matching. The **940 old subscribers are not the problem for Shorts**, but
  they hurt **long-form**, where subscribers see new uploads early: if they came for
  cats/stories and ignore animal-cam compilations, early long-form CTR looks bad.
  [CREDIBLE reasoning; not officially confirmed]
- Channel-level reputation/quality is an official input. A channel with a run of
  0-view Shorts carries no penalty we can prove, but also no positive niche signal.

### Fresh channel or keep this one? — Decision
- **Keep the current channel for the 30-day test** (section 5). Reasons: no proof of a
  channel-level penalty; existing subs help community/comments; the format is still
  animals, so the topic is adjacent.
- **Start a fresh channel only if** the kill criteria in section 5 trigger AND a quick
  A/B check (post the same 5 clips on a new channel, 1 week) clearly beats this one.
  Cross-posting the same clips to two YouTube channels risks reused-content flags, so
  the test channel gets different clips. [Judgment call]

## 2. Long-form ranking (compilations)

- Long-form is clicked, so **CTR (impressions click-through rate) × average view
  duration / % viewed** is the working model; high CTR with low AVD reads as clickbait.
  [CREDIBLE consensus; YouTube says satisfaction matters more than any one metric]
- **Browse (home) vs. Suggested**: a small channel's compilations will mostly live on
  **Suggested** next to other animal-cam/funny-animal videos. Design titles/thumbnails to
  sit beside the top videos in the niche. Home feed traffic needs a proven audience.
- **Home-page squeeze**: a creator analyst (Mario Joos, Dec 2025) documented the home
  page showing far fewer long-form slots and more Shorts. YouTube has not confirmed.
  [UNVERIFIED] → another reason to target Suggested.
- **Shorts → long-form conversion is weak.** Shorts and long-form audiences overlap
  little (claims as low as ~10%) and no study quantifies the related-video link's
  conversion. [UNVERIFIED numbers] Treat Shorts as discovery for the *channel*, not as a
  reliable feeder for compilations.
- **Average view duration is falling platform-wide**: a Metricool study (799,718 videos,
  71,177 accounts) found long-form AVD down 37% and Shorts at 61% of organic views.
  [CREDIBLE, Tubefilter Aug 2026]

### Compilation spec
- **Length: 8-12 min target** (passes 8 min for mid-roll ads once monetized; long enough
  for session time, short enough to hold). Funny-animal compilations of 10-20 min also
  work in the niche; start shorter and lengthen only if retention holds. [Judgment;
  8-min mid-roll threshold is a long-standing YouTube rule]
- **Hook 0-15 s**: open with the single best twist clip, not a logo or "welcome back".
- **Order**: best clip first, second-best last (end-screen window), weakest in the
  middle third. Ascending "rank" framing (#20 → #1) holds viewers to the end.
- **Chapters**: add timestamps (first one at 0:00, at least 3 chapters, each >=10 s —
  YouTube's chapter rules). Name each with the twist hook ("0:42 Raccoon returns the
  package"). Chapters can surface in Search as key moments.
- **End screens**: last 5-20 s; one "best for viewer" video + subscribe. Keep the final
  clip playing under the end screen so viewers don't leave early.
- **Compilation must add value** (reused/inauthentic rules): our clips are original
  generations, which helps, but add a narrated or on-screen-text through-line
  (ranking, captions, running joke, "which one is real?" commentary). Pure
  back-to-back clips with no narrative is the pattern YouTube names as non-monetizable
  for reused clips. [OFFICIAL policy text]

## 3. Monetization + AI policy risk

### Current rules (stack, oldest → newest)
- **Mar 2024 — Altered or synthetic content disclosure** [OFFICIAL]: must disclose
  realistic content that a viewer could mistake for a real person/place/event,
  including "realistic depictions of fictional major events" and altered footage of
  real places. Clearly unrealistic/animated content doesn't need it. Disclosure doesn't
  limit audience or monetization. Non-disclosure → YouTube may add a permanent label;
  repeated failure → removal or YPP suspension. Sensitive topics (news, disasters,
  health, finance, elections) get a more prominent in-player label.
  → Our realistic doorbell/CCTV fakes **need the label**. Keep it ON.
- **Jul 15 2025 — "Repetitious" renamed "Inauthentic content"** [OFFICIAL]: not
  allowed: mass-produced, generic, template-made, same-scenario-same-outcome
  storylines; "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving
  the impression of mass production"; stitching unrelated AI clips to shock. Allowed:
  AI used to visualize your own characters/narratives where the final product reflects
  your creative vision. Applies **channel-wide**.
- **Reused content** (unchanged) [OFFICIAL]: compilations of other people's clips with
  little or no narrative are not monetizable, even with permission. Ours are original
  generations, so this matters only if we ever include third-party footage. Don't.
- **Jan 2026 — CEO letter + AI slop crackdown** [CREDIBLE]: Neal Mohan named reducing
  low-quality AI "slop" a 2026 priority. Days later YouTube removed/hid ~16-18 large AI
  slop channels (tens of millions of subs; per Kapwing, a vendor source).
- **Jul 16 2026 — Inauthentic content clarified into 3 categories** [OFFICIAL via
  TechCrunch, Matt Halprin on Creator Insider]:
  1. Repetitive/templated, easy-to-mass-produce content with little variation.
  2. **Off-putting or distressing content made to manipulate emotions — example: an
     animal in distress that someone then rescues. Channels dedicated to it lose YPP
     whether or not AI was used.**
  3. AI personas of real people discussing finance/legal/health.
  "A channel with too much content from any of these categories will be unable to
  monetize."
- **Staged animal rescue ban** (Community Guidelines, 2021) still applies — this is a
  removal risk, not only a monetization one. [CREDIBLE]

### What gets AI animal channels demonetized vs. what stays safe

| DON'T (high risk) | DO (safer) |
|---|---|
| Animal in danger, attacked, injured, trapped, "rescued" | Animal being cheeky, clever, wholesome, absurd |
| Predator vs pet (leopard and cat, coyote at the door) | Raccoon "returns" package, bear rings doorbell and leaves, dog opens fridge |
| Same setup + same twist every upload ("cat knocks thing off") | Rotate camera type, animal, location, time of day, twist type |
| Fake news framing ("BREAKING: wolf attack in Ohio"), real town names, disasters | Fictional/unnamed locations; no real-event framing |
| Reposting other creators' AI clips or viral CCTV | Only our own generations, our own prompts |
| 20 uploads/day of near-identical clips | 1-3/day, each with a distinct mini-story |
| Hiding that it's AI, "100% REAL" titles | Disclosure ON; optionally play with "real or AI?" in comments |
| AI human presenter giving advice | Characters are animals; any narration is our own script |
| Compilation = clips back-to-back, no glue | Ranked countdown, captions, running joke, recurring "cast" |

**Mitigations that build an "authentic" record**: recurring named characters (e.g., a
named raccoon that keeps returning), a consistent channel voice in captions, our own
scripted narration on long-form, varied outcomes. Uncertain how YouTube's classifier
weighs these, but they map directly to the policy's "distinct storyline/concept"
allowance. [Judgment]

**Engaged-views requirement**: YPP Shorts path = 1,000 subs + 10M valid public Shorts
views in 90 days (or 4,000 watch hours/12 mo). Lower fan-funding tier (500 subs) exists
in many countries — verify thresholds in Studio → Earn. [OFFICIAL/CREDIBLE]

## 4. Viral mechanics for "caught on camera" animal clips

### Hook (0-1 s)
- Frame 1 = motion + a clear subject. Cam UI overlay (timestamp, "REC", camera name)
  sells the format instantly — keep it, it's our "brand frame".
- On-screen text in first second, <=6 words, sets an expectation the twist breaks:
  "He thought no one was watching…", "Wait for the porch light".
- Don't open on an empty porch. Cut the first 1-2 s of every generation.

### Loop
- 8-15 s is right for this format. End on a frame that matches/flows into frame 1
  (same camera angle, animal exits the way it entered), so replays feel seamless.
  Loops add views and completion. [CREDIBLE]
- Twist lands at ~70-85% of runtime, then 1-2 s reaction beat, then cut. No outro.
- Length note: vidIQ's large study found 13 s and ~60 s as sweet spots. 13 s fits us.
  [CREDIBLE-ish, vendor study]

### Title formulas (Shorts titles show in feed less, but matter for Search/related)
- `[Animal] tried [normal thing]… then [twist]` → "Raccoon tried to steal a package… then this"
- `Doorbell cam caught [animal] doing [unexpected]`
- `Nobody expected the [animal] to [twist]`
- `[Animal] didn't know the camera was on`
- Keep <60 chars, put the animal word first (search + context). No "REAL", no
  "BREAKING", no real places.

### Comment bait (organic, policy-safe)
- End caption question: "What was he looking for?", "Name this raccoon", "Real or AI? 👀"
  (the disclosure label answers it; the debate still drives comments).
- Pin a comment with a recurring-character lore line; reply to early comments fast
  (first hour).
- Avoid "like if…/comment 1 if…" engagement bait (TikTok/Meta penalize; YouTube
  disfavors manipulative tactics).

### Posting frequency & timing
- **Shorts: 1-2/day** for the 30-day test (2/day at 10 AM + 4 PM ET is fine). More only
  if each clip is genuinely distinct — volume of near-identical clips is exactly the
  inauthentic pattern. Channels with 200+ Shorts tend to see rising views
  (vidIQ). [CREDIBLE-ish]
- **Compilation: 1/week**, same day/time (e.g., Saturday 10 AM ET), built from that
  week's top-retention Shorts **plus 30%+ unseen clips** so it isn't pure re-upload.
- Time of day matters little for Shorts (feed tests over days); check Studio →
  Audience → "When your viewers are on YouTube" after 2 weeks and adjust Metricool.

### Shorts → long-form funnel
- Link the weekly compilation as the **Related video** on every Short of that week.
- Shorts end text occasionally: "Full #1 in this week's compilation".
- Expect low conversion (see section 2). Measure: Studio → compilation → Traffic source
  "Shorts feed"/"Related video". If <5% of compilation views come from Shorts after 4
  weeks, stop optimizing for the funnel. [Threshold is our heuristic]

### Cross-posting TikTok / Reels
- Upload **clean, watermark-free masters** natively to each platform. Instagram
  deprioritizes visibly recycled clips (TikTok watermark) in Reels recommendations;
  clean native uploads aren't penalized. [OFFICIAL Instagram/Mosseri]
- Turn on each platform's AI label: TikTok requires labels on realistic AI content and
  auto-reads C2PA content credentials (Veo/Sora outputs may carry them); Meta shows
  "AI info". TikTok is testing a user toggle to see less AI content. [CREDIBLE]
- TikTok/Reels are the faster test bed: a clip that wins there is a strong candidate to
  re-edit (new hook text) for YouTube, and vice versa.

## 5. Escaping the 0-view trap — 30-day plan

### Days 1-3: Reset & hygiene
- [ ] Studio: confirm not "Made for kids", no region blocks, Shorts are 9:16 and <=3 min.
- [ ] Confirm Metricool uploads arrive as Shorts (vertical, appear in Shorts tab).
- [ ] Rewrite channel name/handle description, banner and avatar to say what the
      channel *is now*: "Animals caught on camera doing the unexpected (AI-made)".
- [ ] Organize old cat/story videos into a separate playlist; consider unlisting
      0-view ones that confuse topic (unproven benefit; low cost). [UNVERIFIED]
- [ ] Create playlists: "Doorbell cam", "Dashcam animals", "Weekly compilations".

### Days 4-30: Run the test
- 2 Shorts/day, every one distinct (rotate: animal × camera × location × twist type).
- Tag every upload in a log: camera type, animal, twist category, hook text, length.
- 1 compilation/week from day 8.
- Post the same masters (clean) to TikTok + Reels as a parallel signal.
- Reply to every comment within the first hour of each upload.
- Review weekly (not daily) — per clip: Viewed %, avg % viewed, engaged views,
  subs gained. Double down on the top 20% by Viewed %; drop bottom 20% categories.

### Benchmarks (our targets; third-party rules of thumb, not official)
| Metric | Bad | OK | Good |
|---|---|---|---|
| Viewed (vs swiped) | <50% | 60-70% | 75%+ |
| Avg % viewed (8-15 s Short) | <70% | 80-100% | 100%+ (loops) |
| Shorts engaged views median, wk 3-4 | <100 | 300-1,000 | 1,000+ |
| Compilation CTR | <2% | 3-5% | 6%+ |
| Compilation avg % viewed (10 min) | <25% | 30-40% | 45%+ |

Reference: new channels' Shorts median ~1,300-1,400 views per upload, best-of-first-10
median ~8,800 (vidIQ 2026, channels created Jan-Jun 2025). We're far below — so the
problem is distribution/hook, not "too few uploads".

### Kill / pivot criteria (decide on day 30, or earlier if triggered)
- **Pivot the content** if after 40+ distinct Shorts: median engaged views <100 AND
  Viewed % <55% on most clips. → Hooks/format aren't landing; change camera
  styles/animals/twist types before changing channels.
- **Try a fresh channel** if Viewed % is decent (65%+) but impressions stay tiny
  (YouTube isn't testing us at all) AND the same clips do clearly better on
  TikTok/Reels. → Points to channel-level placement, not content.
- **Kill the format** if TikTok/Reels/YouTube all show <100 views median after 40+
  clips — the format itself isn't working.
- **Stop immediately** if: any "inauthentic content" / reused content YPP notice,
  any Community Guidelines strike, or YouTube auto-applies AI labels we didn't set
  (means our disclosure isn't sticking).
- **Scale** if any clip passes 10k engaged views: make 3-5 *variations of the idea*
  (same character, new twist), not copies.

## 6. Per-upload checklist

Before generating
- [ ] Twist is funny/wholesome/absurd, **no animal in danger, pain, attack, or rescue**.
- [ ] Not the same animal + same twist as any of the last 10 uploads.
- [ ] No real people's likenesses, no real place names, no news framing.

Edit
- [ ] Frame 1 has motion + subject; first 1-2 s of dead air trimmed.
- [ ] Cam overlay (timestamp/REC) present; 9:16; 8-15 s.
- [ ] Hook text <=6 words in first second; twist at 70-85%; ending loops into start.
- [ ] Captions readable on mobile, inside safe zones (avoid bottom 20% / right edge).

Upload (Metricool → YouTube)
- [ ] Title: animal word first, <60 chars, no "REAL"/"BREAKING".
- [ ] **Altered or synthetic content = Yes** (confirm in Studio after Metricool posts;
      if the scheduler can't set it, set it manually in Studio before publish).
- [ ] Description: 1 line story + "AI-generated" + 2-3 hashtags (#doorbellcam #animals).
- [ ] Related video = this week's compilation (Studio → Shorts → Related video).
- [ ] Not made for kids; correct category (Pets & Animals or Comedy).
- [ ] Pin comment with question/lore.

Cross-post
- [ ] Clean master (no other platform's watermark) to TikTok + Reels, AI label ON.

After 48 h and 7 d
- [ ] Log Viewed %, avg % viewed, engaged views, subs. Don't delete underperformers.

Compilation extras
- [ ] Best clip first; narrated/captioned through-line; 30%+ unseen clips.
- [ ] Chapters (0:00 first, 3+, each >=10 s); end screen last 5-20 s; 8-12 min.
- [ ] Thumbnail: one big animal face + cam overlay + 2-4 words; test 2-3 with
      YouTube's thumbnail A/B "Test & compare" if available.
