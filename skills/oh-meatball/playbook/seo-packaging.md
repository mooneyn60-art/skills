# Oh Meatball: SEO and Packaging Bank

Format: realistic AI-generated "caught on camera" animal clips (doorbell, security, trail, dash and phone footage). Shorts run 8-15 s. Weekly Shorts compilations run 1-3 min, and long-form compilations run 8-12 min.
Last research pass: 2026-10-19.

---

## 0. Ground rules (read first)

1. **Disclose AI every time.** Turn on YouTube Studio's "Altered or synthetic content" toggle for every upload. The format is realistic footage a viewer could take as real, which is exactly what the policy targets. Creators who repeatedly skip the label risk removal or YPP suspension. Also put "AI-generated" in the first two lines of the description and keep `#aianimals` among the hashtags.
   - **The label does not hurt reach.** The 2.4M-on-2.8k-subs outlier was AI-labeled.
   - **Put the twist in the title, not a claim that the clip is real.** Never write "REAL", "not fake" or "100% real footage". Fact-checkers (Lead Stories, PetaPixel) already debunk this format, and a debunk article plus a "fake" comment storm will cost you more than any boost.
2. **Avoid the "inauthentic content" flag (July 2025 rename of "repetitious content").** Each compilation needs real added value: a numbered countdown, on-screen captions, chapter titles, a recurring narrator or text voice ("Oh Meatball rating: 9/10 chaos"), and themed sequencing. Never auto-stitch.
3. **Never use real brand marks.** No Ring, Nest, Arlo, Blink or Tesla logos in frames, titles or tags. "Doorbell cam" and "security camera" are generic. A brand name used as a tag is misleading-metadata risk, and it implies the footage came from that company's device.
4. **Stay on realistic behavior.** No costumes, no talking, no human injuries, no animals actually being harmed. Chaos is fine; gore is not.

## 4. Thumbnails for long-form compilations

### 4a. What wins in this niche (pattern read of the 1M+ outliers and the big compilation channels)
- **Composition:** one hero frame, never a collage of 6 tiny clips. Use a 2-panel split at most: big animal plus a smaller "reaction" element. The hero animal fills 40-60% of the frame, placed off-center (rule of thirds), facing into the frame.
- **Sell the camera look.** Keep a fake CCTV overlay: a REC dot, a timestamp ("03:12 AM"), a slight fisheye, IR black-and-white, or a doorbell vignette. The overlay is the genre signal that tells browse viewers "caught on camera" in under 0.2 s. No brand logos.
- **Faces:** top channels pair the animal with a surprised human face (homeowner in the doorway, hands on head). For us, use an AI-generated human with a shocked expression, or the animal's own "caught" face (raccoon frozen mid-steal, eyes glowing in IR). Animal eyes looking at the lens beat profile shots.
- **Text:** 2-4 words, high-contrast sans (Impact, Bebas, Montserrat Black), white or yellow with a thick black stroke. Place it top-left or bottom-right, never over the animal's face. The text should add to the title, not repeat it (title "30 Most Unpredictable…", thumbnail "HE CAME BACK").
- **Arrows and circles:** one red circle or arrow at most, pointing at the "thing" (the stolen package, the second bear in the shadows). They still lift CTR in the compilation genre, but more than one reads as spam.
- **Color:** desaturated or IR CCTV background, with the subject and text in saturated contrast (yellow text, a red circle, a warm-lit animal). Bright yellow, red and white are the dominant accents in this genre. Avoid an all-gray IR thumbnail with nothing popping.
- **Readability test:** check the thumbnail at 168×94 px (the mobile suggested-video size). If the animal and its action aren't obvious at that size, simplify.
- **Consistency:** use the same corner badge on every compilation (a small meatball icon plus "VOL. 3") to build series recognition.

### 4b. Five concepts for the first long-form ("30 Most Unpredictable Animal Moments Caught on Camera (AI)")
1. **"HE CAME BACK":** night-vision doorbell frame, a raccoon carrying a package away, with a second pair of glowing eyes circled in red in the bushes. REC dot, 03:12 AM timestamp. Yellow text top-left.
2. **"NOT THE POOL GUY":** daylight backyard cam, a black bear sitting upright in a blue pool like a person, a homeowner silhouette frozen at the patio door (small, bottom-right). Red arrow to the bear. White text.
3. **"WHERE'S MY SHOE?":** porch-cam fisheye, a goose waddling off with one sneaker; the barefoot owner hopping in the doorway, mouth open. Bright daylight, saturated grass. Text bottom-left.
4. **"4:07 AM…":** security cam over an empty ice rink, a walrus mid-slide on the ice, a Zamboni in the background. Cold blue tones with yellow text; the timestamp itself is the hook. No arrow needed.
5. **"WHO DID THIS?":** 2-panel split. Left: a pristine lawn ("BEFORE" label). Right: craters with a muddy golden retriever staring straight into the lens. Red circle on the dog's dirty paws.

Test #1 vs #2 vs #5 in Test & Compare. Expected winner: #2 (biggest animal, clearest absurdity at small size).

### 5d. Hashtag rules
- **Count:** use 3 in the description, 5 at most. The first 3 display above the title. More than 15 makes YouTube ignore them all, and 6+ starts to look spammy.
- **Shorts:** `#shorts` is no longer required for classification (vertical and ≤3 min is enough). It is optional; include it as the last hashtag at most.
- **Title hashtags:** 0-2 niche ones (`#doorbellcam`, `#plotwist`). They act as clickable discovery hubs; the 4000x outlier used two.
- **Which:** one camera tag (`#doorbellcam`, `#securitycamera`, `#dashcam`, `#trailcam`), one niche tag (`#animalscaughtoncamera`, `#caughtoncamera`), one AI tag (`#aianimals`, which is honest and builds an AI-genre audience), plus one animal tag if room (`#raccoon`, `#bear`, `#goose`). Seasonal: `#halloween` and `#christmas` in the 2 weeks before the date only.
- **Never:** `#fyp`, `#viral` (no function on YouTube), brand tags (`#ring`, `#nest`), or `#real`/`#notfake`.
