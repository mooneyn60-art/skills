## 1. Why our long-forms get no impressions

- **YouTube "pulls" videos for viewers. It does not push them for creators.** The recommendation signals it names are viewing behaviour, likes, dislikes, subscriptions and satisfaction surveys, plus channel reputation. https://www.youtube.com/howyoutubeworks/recommendations/ · Ritchie: "doesn't and never did push videos for creators" (via search summary of his blog, wording unverified) https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/renes-top-five-on-youtube-november-2-2023-edition/
- **"Tested on a small audience first, then widened" is creator folklore.** I found no primary YouTube source that confirms it (UNVERIFIED). Third-party guides say small channels get most early views from **search**, not browse. https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-metrics-small-channels-growth · https://smashballoon.com/youtube-algorithm-for-small-channels/
- **Shorts viewers do not carry over by default.** A YouTube rep on Creator Insider said "we separate Shorts and long-form content from watch history … not currently using that to inform what longer videos are recommended" (2022; no newer primary reversal found). https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-shorts-algorithm/543939/ Metricool's 2026 study (a vendor) also says the two recommenders run separately (research/60). **Our 1-2K-view Shorts give our long-forms nothing unless a viewer taps through.**
- **The only official bridge is the Related video link,** plus a spoken and visual CTA in the Short's last 5 s. The long-form's first 5-10 s must pay off the Short's promise, with no logos or intro. https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/ (2026-07-14)
- **CTR and retention are the gates.** YouTube Help: "Half of all channels and videos … between 2% and 10%" CTR, and new videos and videos under 100 views swing wider. High CTR with low AVD reads as clickbait and earns "lower-than-expected impressions". https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154 Our CTR (1.2-2.65%) sits at or under the floor of that band. Our 11-16% viewed means a ~10-min video loses viewers at ~1-1.5 min. Each weak signal gives YouTube no reason to show the video to more people. **Inference:** the low CTR is the bigger problem, because almost nobody sees the video in the first place.
- **Topic is not the problem, and Conspiracies proves it.** The Paint Explainer's "Every Insane Conspiracy Theory That Turned Out to Be True" has 2,993,795 views; ours has 2. Same topic, so the gap is packaging plus zero channel signal. (yt-dlp search, 11/2)

## RULES FOR ALL LONG-FORMS

1. **Title:** a specific actor or animal + verb + absurd object, or "You/Every" framing. Include the search term people type. No generic "Weird Facts" or "Everything You Know Is WRONG" lists.
2. **Thumbnail:** one subject readable at 168 px, ≤3 words naming the stakes, Meatball's face kept. Case files also get one real archive photo or stamp. Always check the 168 px version before shipping.
3. **First 30 s:** pay off the title in the first 5-10 s. No jokes or comment asks before the story starts (target 0:25). Set one open loop and pay it off at the end. Target 60%+ still watching at 30 s.
4. **Every long-form ships with a matching Shorts teaser** cut from its own cold open, with a Related link and a last-5-s CTA. A Short must never promise what the long-form contradicts.
5. **Test & Compare on every upload** (3 titles or 2-3 thumbnails). Keep the winner's pattern.
6. **Run 10-15 min** in compilations; case files can stay at ~8-10 min (Dark Docs' 7:55 Azorian has 1.41M views).
7. **Pick angles the big videos skip** on crowded topics (Azorian: fern 5.57M). Check `yt-dlp --flat-playlist "ytsearch10:<topic>"` before scripting.
8. **No premieres** until the channel has an audience that would turn up live. Publish at a consistent time.
