# 88 - Fact-check of the house cat long-form script (2026-11-12)

A second model (not the writer) checked `scripts/longform-born-as-house-cat.outline.json` line by line against the live sources: every spoken line, card and picture note, plus the titles and the verdict. It opened the pages itself (PMC full texts, abstracts through the Europe PMC API, the FDA, ASPCA, Cornell and Merck pages, the New York bill PDF). Facts: `research/87-house-cat-life-factpack.md`.

Result: not safe to build as first written. About 45 of 128 lines were softened or re-attributed, and **two numbers in the fact pack itself were misread** and had been carried into the script. All fixes are applied (the outline's `_factcheck` field says so).

## The two fact-pack misreads
1. **Kidney disease (HC-D7, HC-D9).** The pack said 61.3% of senior cats at a US vet school had kidney disease. In Herrera 2026 that 61.3% (49 of 80) is the share of kidney-disease cats whose disease was newly found. The prevalence was 80 of 104 staged cats (77%), early stages counted. And "75.6% had no owner-reported change" is no change in urination habits (34 of 45), not no change at all. Script now: "About 77 percent of the senior cats staged at one American vet school, early stages counted" and "three in four owners had seen no change in their peeing".
2. **Window falls (HC-E6).** "96.5% survived" (115 of 119) counts six cats that were put down as survivors, four of them because of the cost of treatment; four more died within 36 hours. 109 of 119 lived. Script now: "109 of them lived. Four died. Six were put down, four of those because of the cost of treatment."

Also wrong in the pack: the Merck quote under HC-A12 is no longer on the live page (the page says kittens "handled daily by people during the first month of life have been found to ... be more outgoing, be more social to humans"); HC-H4's "cats appear to be exploiting" sentence is the university news release's own text, not the lead researcher's words; HC-E12 names "ribbon", which the paper does not; HC-E7's call for window barriers is the Berlin study (1,125 cats), not the Zagreb one (119).

## Other fixes, by kind
- **Hedges put back:** "probably" a few siblings; "basically" a wildcat; cake "probably" wasted; food "back then was probably" breaking hearts; "may have hacked" your human; "a safe house could win on years"; kittens "have been found to be" more social.
- **Who said it:** "Merck's vet manual calls it..."; "A 2007 paper cites cat and human remains found together on Cyprus" (second-hand); "A Cornell vet says" (one vet); "The university's news release says"; "The ASPCA estimates"; "In a second study of English cats that died".
- **What the number is a share of:** two cats in the hearing test; six and five cats in the neutering study; 165 of 362 deaths on roads and 41 of 67 kitten deaths (not "almost half of cats"); 92 of 100 on X-ray ("X-ray signs", not pain); the yearly death chance is "recorded", "up to age eight", and a minimum; the Bristol study "didn't record which ones went outside"; feral colonies were managed colonies and "not pet-cat odds".
- **Safety lines:** the lily lines keep "can" and get their trigger ("if you might have touched one, your human calls a vet, right away"; card "THINK IT TOUCHED ONE? CALL A VET NOW"); a call-a-vet line follows the acetaminophen line; the hairball line names the signs an owner can see (tired, not eating for a day or two, retching with nothing coming up); no swear word in any safety line ("needs an asterisk", not "a damn asterisk"); the carrier joke ends "Go anyway."
- **Pictures:** the cat never eats the cake; no distance on the blurry toy; a pill bottle, not one pill; listeners mark rating cards, they do not reach for cat food; the dog by the lily has a mild tummy ache (the FDA's "minor stomach upset"), not a shrug; no mop scene after the milk.
- **Declawing:** the line quoting the bill sponsor's memo was replaced by the bill's own wording (declawing is listed with phalangectomy and tendonectomy), because the checker could not open the memo.
- **Verdict and titles:** the verdict now names its conditions ("if your human keeps the house safe and reads the damn manual"; the five pillars "plus a safe house and vet visits"), because the pillars alone do not cover lilies, pills, windows or vet visits, and no source compares indoor with outdoor life. Titles: "rigged" dropped; "8 Ways Being a House Cat Is a Raw Deal (And Why It's Still Worth It)" added, since the video ends on a conditional sweet deal.

## Left open
- "Sucks" in title 1 needs Nolan's yes (mild-word rule for titles).
- New York's 2019 signing date: read by the fact-pack writer on the Senate site, not re-read by the checker (the site put up a wall).
- Ellis 2015 and Soennichsen 2002 (petting), McComb 2009 (purr), Fettman 1997 (neutering weight): abstracts or a university release only.
- Vigne 2004 (Cyprus) is second-hand through Driscoll 2007.

## Lessons
1. The checker must open the paper, not the pack: both misreads were in the pack's own "safe wording" column.
2. A percentage needs its denominator in the sentence (of deaths, of cats staged, of cats brought to the clinic).
3. A survival figure needs the count of animals put down.
4. A safety line needs the trigger a viewer can act on, not only the consequence.
