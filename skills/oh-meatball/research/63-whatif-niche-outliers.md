## 2. Patterns that repeat (evidence count = outliers above showing it)

- **Hook wording (first 1–3 s)** — the title IS the spoken first line, phrased as a question. "What if…"
  opener: 11/19. "Why/How [animal] [does something to] you": 5 (Zack D. horseflies/spiders/ticks/cockroaches/
  Dobermans). Second person "you/your" in the title: 13/19. Zack D. titles end in a reaction emoji 😨/😵/😞: 5/5.
  Hooks are short: a study of 1,178 million-view Shorts found a median hook of 11 words (overseeros.com).
- **Topic types** — (a) *removal*: "what if every X vanished/disappeared", 6 (mosquito ×4, spiders, humans);
  (b) *body invasion / bites / parasites*: 9 (tapeworm ×2, ticks, horseflies, mosquito bites ×2, spider inside
  you, zombie snails, botfly); (c) *scale-up*: 4 (tiger-sized kitten, human-sized ants, spiders as big as cars,
  1cm every second); (d) *house invaders*: 4 (house spiders, dust, cockroaches, termites 13M). Mosquitoes are the
  single most repeated outlier animal (5 entries, 412K–17M).
- **Escalation structure** — a number in the premise that keeps climbing or a hard limit: "1 million
  mosquitoes", "1cm every second", "1,000 baths", "10 times", "100 sneezes": 7. Twist suffix "(But There's a
  Catch)" lifted Hypothetica's spider and mosquito episodes to 3.5–3.8x its median: 2. Timeline order (Day/Week/
  Year) is the classic long-form shape (Infinite Comparison "Timeline: What If You Never Showered" 547K).
- **Length** — known lengths of outliers: 0:30, 0:34, 0:40, 0:47, 0:49, 0:51, 0:52 → most YouTube winners run
  30–52 s (7). Over 60 s works for story-driven entries: VoidCast 2:08 (2.4M in 4 days), PolyRelic 2:09 (7.8x): 2.
- **Shot pace** — viral Shorts usually have ≥2 cuts in the first 3 s and 81% carry on-screen text (general
  Shorts study via clipspeed/overseeros, not niche-specific). Niche-specific cut rate: (unverified).
- **Art style and colors** — 3D (Zack D., MrSkelly "#3d", Professor Of How 16.2M subs) and flat vector
  (Kurzgesagt, BioChok, Vin Explains) both produce outliers; stickman did not in this sample. Bright saturated
  palette on dark backgrounds is the Kurzgesagt look (unverified for the others).
- **Narrator voice** — single male narrator, calm/deadpan, explaining to "you" (Zack D., Kurzgesagt, What If)
  (unverified, from format knowledge, not measured).
- **Music** — low, steady tension bed under the voice rather than loud drops (unverified; BPM not measurable here).
- **Captions** — word-by-word burned-in captions are standard on faceless Shorts; 81% of viral Shorts have
  text (general study). Niche-specific style: (unverified).
- **On-screen counters** — counter in the *title* in 7 outliers (see escalation). An on-screen DAYS/number
  counter is likely but was not frame-checked (unverified).
- **End card / Part 2 / comment bait** — "pt.2" in title on a 37.9M Day-vs-Year Short; "Part 5/6" naming on
  never-showered clones did NOT help (420–1.1K views). Part 2 works only after Part 1 already hits: 1 positive,
  2 negative.
- **Posting cadence** — Zack D. has 4,109 YouTube videos (vidIQ) and 2,848 TikToks (TokPortal); VoidCast,
  Hypothetica and BioChok each showed 26–48 Shorts on their tab. Exact per-week cadence: (unverified).
- **Series naming** — winners use a repeatable title *template*, not a series label: "What If Every X
  Disappeared? (But There's a Catch)" (Hypothetica), "You Only Get N ___" (VoidCast), "Why/How [animal] ___ 😨"
  (Zack D.), "Timeline: What If You ___" (WatchData/Infinite Comparison): 4 channels.

## 3. What to change in "What If You Never Cleaned Your House?"

1. **Make it a bug video, not a hygiene video.** Human-hygiene "what if" clones are saturated (most under
   7K views). Recast it as "What If You Never Cleaned Your House? (The Bugs Move In)": each counter jump brings
   a new animal — dust mites (Kurzgesagt dust 3M), silverfish, cockroaches (Zack D. 14M; "It's just a
   cockroach" 38M), spiders eating the cockroaches (Zack D. house spiders 12M), mice, then termites (13M).
   This is our niche and the escalation has a real food-chain logic.
2. **Use V2 (flat navy vector, faceless "you"), retire V1 stick-Meatball for this format.** Flat vector has
   multiple outliers (Kurzgesagt, BioChok, Vin Explains); stickman had none in this sample. Keep Meatball as a
   small mascot cameo or end-card sign-off so the brand stays.
3. **Front-load the payoff.** Open on the Day-365 frame (the house is crawling) for about 1 s, then
   "Day 1." Spoken hook ≤11 words: "What if you never cleaned your house again? Day 1…"
4. **Put the number in the title and on screen.** The DAYS counter is right; make it big, top-center, and let
   each jump land on a cut. Consider bug-count as a second counter ("Roaches: 1 → 12,000").
5. **Two cuts by length.** TikTok: 61–65 s (Creator Rewards needs 60 s+; 72 s is fine but trim dead air).
   YouTube Shorts: a 45–50 s edit, since known outlier lengths cluster at 30–52 s.
6. **End on a question, not "Part 2".** Use "Which bug moved in first at YOUR place?" or a cliff ("…but on day
   366, something ate the spiders"). Only make Part 2 when Part 1 beats our median by 3x or more.
7. **Title emoji + second person.** e.g. "What If You Never Cleaned Your House Again? 🪳😨".
8. **Facts must be true.** Kurzgesagt, Zack D. and BioChok all frame "what if" as fact delivery. Keep each
   counter beat to a checkable fact (dust-mite and roach breeding numbers), since that matches "Meatball Myths".

## 5. Series name and posting plan

**Series name:** "What If… (Bug Edition)" as the title template, with "Meatball Myths" in the handle/end card.
Format each title as "What If [you + animal + number]? [emoji]" and use "(But There's a Catch)" on removal
topics. Avoid "Part N" in titles unless Part 1 has broken out.

**TikTok (9.4K followers, Creator Rewards, 60 s+):**
- 61–70 s cuts with the DAYS counter; 4–5 posts/week (unverified best cadence — no niche cadence data found).
- Hook text on screen in frame 1, and the spoken question by 1.5 s.
- Pin a comment asking "which day would you leave?" to drive replies (unverified effect).
- Expect lower ceilings than YouTube: even Zack D. averages 213K on TikTok vs a 7.4M YT Shorts median (TokPortal).
- Run topics 1, 2, 3, 7 first (strongest proof); re-post winners' structure with new animals.

**YouTube Shorts (~970 subs):**
- Separate 40–52 s edits; YouTube winners here are mostly under a minute. Long story edits (2:00+) can still
  work, as VoidCast (2:08, 2.4M in 4 days) and PolyRelic (2:09, 7.8x) show.
- Title = spoken hook + emoji; the title carries more weight in YouTube search than on TikTok. Many outliers
  above surfaced through plain "what if [animal]" searches.
- Small channels can break out on YouTube: BioChok (4.66K subs, 208x), Hypothetica (796 subs, 3.5x),
  PolyRelic (640 subs, 7.8x). Track each Short's views against our own median, and only spin off a series
  from those at 3x or more.
