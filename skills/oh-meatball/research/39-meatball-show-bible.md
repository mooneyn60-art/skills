# 39 — The Meatball show bible (The Showrunner, 2026-10-24)

**Brief (Nolan).** Meatball is the brand and stays the same. Make him funnier and more watchable. Two attempts failed: the hyper "punch" style (shakes, zooms, SFX: "too much") and a bouncing mascot.
**Answer in one line.** Keep the calm picture and change the *writing*. Meatball becomes a deadpan nature narrator who happens to be a meatball, so he takes every mention of eating personally. The comedy comes from the gap between his calm voice and what he says. It does not come from motion or noise.

**Legend.** [M] measured here (vidIQ transcripts and watches, yt-dlp metadata, 2026-10-24). [I] my inference. Credits spent: **85 of 120** (6 transcripts at 5 = 30; 2 outlier searches at 5 = 10; 1 long-form watch at 25; 2 Short watches at 10 = 20).

### 1b. How the jokes are built [M + I]
1. **Clinical setup, blunt undercut.** True Facts uses about 4-8 s of documentary wording, then ends the sentence on a crude word: "many shapes, colours and shades… of ugly". There is a **0.5-1.0 s pause before the punch word**. This is the core move for us.
2. **Misreading the picture.** The narrator describes the footage as a human social ritual: the angler's lure becomes "a lovely pashmina shawl… a pair of leggings… a decorative hat feather". Each one is a new punchline on the same image, so no extra shots are needed.
3. **Rule of three plus a repeated line.** Sam O'Nella says "so most of the emus got away" three times; the repetition is the joke. OverSimplified repeats "More economic downturn!" and the feather-hat slip ("Did I say feather hat?").
4. **A metaphor that escalates.** Casually Explained starts as a straight textbook, then slips one game term in, then piles them up, and ends on "the whole process could be cancelled by pressing B". The straight start makes the turn funnier.
5. **Asides and footnotes.** "Let me Google it", "I'm not joking, that's actually real" (platypus), and True Facts' end-card text ("My deepest sympathies to the male angler fish"). This is a second voice inside the narrator's head, and it costs nothing to produce.
6. **The ending lands on a callback**, never on an outro (the "pretty pretty lady" callback; "better fences").
7. **Calm channels still change the picture every 3-6 s** (True Facts) or every 1.5-3 s (What Gives?, research/21). The picture moves along even though the tone stays calm.

### 1c. What this means for Meatball [I]
- The biggest example of the "funny calm animal narrator" (True Facts, 24M views on a 2-minute video) uses **no stings, no shakes and no zooms**. Nolan's taste is not a handicap; it is the proven format.
- What we lack is **a point of view**. Our current lines state facts and then a pun card ("Stripes all the way down"). The rivals that people love *judge* the animal and *relate* it to themselves.
- Shorts outliers show about 1 gag every 4-5 s. Our current Shorts have **about 1 joke in 20 s** (tiger: "Please do not test this one yourself").

## 2. Persona: three options, one pick

### Option A: "The Narrator Who Is Food" — PICKED
A calm nature-documentary narrator who is, unfortunately, a meatball. He is a professional; he reads the facts straight. But every predator, mouth, appetite or digestion fact lands on him personally, and he cannot quite hide it. He is never loud, only quietly and increasingly concerned.
1. "The lion sleeps up to twenty hours a day. The other four, it eats. I've done the maths. I don't love it."
2. "The pelican can swallow a whole fish. Or, and I'm just raising this, something round."
3. "Sharks can smell blood from far away. Good news: I don't have any. Bad news: I have sauce."
4. "The snake unhinges its jaw to swallow prey whole. I'll be narrating this one from further away."
5. "Every tiger's stripes are different, like a fingerprint. So if one eats you, you'll know exactly who did it."
6. "The cockroach can live a week without its head. I lose one bite and they call it dinner."
7. "Goats will eat almost anything. 'Almost.' I'm clinging to that word."
8. "The python can go months without a meal. Months. Then it has one. Which is the part I think about."
9. "Raccoons wash their food before eating it. Very thoughtful. I'd still prefer not to be clean."
10. "The anteater eats thirty thousand ants a day. Not one meatball. I've checked. I'd like this on the record."

### Option B: "The Overconfident Expert"
Meatball commits to a guess before every reveal and is confidently wrong about a third of the time; the animals win. (This is research/22 idea 1.)
1. "Easy. FALSE. Bats can see fine. … They can. Of course they can. I knew that."
2. "I've been wrong twice this week. Statistically, I'm due."
3. "Ostriches don't bury their heads. I said that. I definitely said that before the answer."
4. "Goldfish remember for months. Months! Longer than I remember this episode's score."
5. "My guess: false. Confidence: total. Track record: let's not."
6. "The bull doesn't care about red. I would like to apologise to the colour red."
7. "Meatball, four. Animals, nine. It's a rebuilding year."
8. "I studied for this. I studied the wrong animal, but I studied."
9. "Porcupines can't shoot quills. I'm going to need everyone to stop looking at me."
10. "In my defence, the myth was very convincing. In the animal's defence, it's true."

### Option C: "The Unimpressed Critic"
Meatball reviews each animal like a restaurant critic giving stars, in a dry, picky voice.
1. "The sloth. Slow service, but the atmosphere is unbeatable. Three stars."
2. "The platypus. Clearly designed by committee. Venomous, egg-laying, duck-faced. Two stars, no notes, many questions."
3. "The flamingo eats shrimp until it turns pink. Commitment to the theme. Five stars."
4. "The goldfish. Remembers you for months. Brings nothing to the table. Two stars."
5. "Koalas sleep twenty hours a day on a diet that's barely food. Relatable. Four stars."
6. "The mantis shrimp punches harder than a bullet. Overkill for lunch. Respect."
7. "The bat: sees fine, chooses not to. Admirable."
8. "The turtle cannot leave its shell. I'll be honest, neither could I after this review."
9. "Cockroach: survives a week headless. A week. Some restaurants don't last that long."
10. "The hyena: great laugh, terrible manners. Three and a half."

### Why A [I]
- **It is the brand.** Only a meatball can have this joke. The name, the drawing and the sauce cap all set it up, so the persona deepens the mascot instead of replacing it.
- **It never runs out.** Almost every animal fact touches food, mouths, predators or digestion, so the angle works in every episode without forcing it.
- **It is calm by construction.** The voice is a documentary narrator, as in True Facts. The joke is understatement ("I don't love it"), so there is nothing to shout.
- **It is fair to the facts.** The fact is read straight first and the joke comes after, so accuracy is never traded for a laugh (research/30 policy risk).
- **B and C survive as bits.** Meatball can still guess wrong now and then (B) and give the odd "Threat level" rating (a cousin of C) without either becoming the identity.

## 3. Catchphrases and verbal habits
- **"I don't love it."** He says it when a fact gets too close to being about him. Use it at most once per Short.
- **"Not a snack."** It is also a small aside card (see 5). It is the merch line [I].
- **"For the record."** He says it before something defensive ("For the record, I'm mostly breadcrumbs.").
- **"Unfortunately."** One word after a sentence that ends well for the animal and badly for him.
- **The reveal opens on the verdict, as now:** "True." / "False." Then one plain sentence of fact. The joke comes *after* the fact, never in place of it.

## 4. Running gags (callbacks across episodes)
1. **The Fork.** It is a silent, still, unexplained fork that stands upright next to Meatball whenever a line is about eating. It never moves and is never mentioned. It just turns up, with the one sting (section 7). After about five episodes viewers will comment "the fork" before it appears [I]. **Built:** the `"prop": "fork"` beat field.
2. **Threat Level card.** One text card per episode: "THREAT TO MEATBALLS: LOW." with the aside "(suspiciously low)". The verdict changes each time ("NONE. IT EATS ANTS." / "HIGH. IT EATS EVERYTHING."). It costs nothing: it is an existing text card plus an aside.
3. **Asides.** A small grey typed footnote in parentheses: his inner voice, which is read, not spoken. It adds a second joke on the same beat without adding seconds. **Built:** the `"aside"` field.
4. **"Who's eating whom."** He keeps a list of animals that eat meatballs ("the official list"), which grows by one entry per episode. Long-form only: a running total in the outro ("The list now has 14 names. Raccoon, you know what you did.").
5. **The Spaghetti Question.** Meatball never talks about spaghetti. If an animal fact mentions noodles, worms or anything long and thin, he goes quiet for a beat and changes the subject. It is a slow-burn mystery for long-form viewers, at most one per episode [I].

## 5. Side characters (optional; Meatball stays central)
- **The Fork** (above): the main one. It never speaks.
- **Fact-Check Pigeon** (long-form only, at most 2 lines per episode). When Meatball oversimplifies, a pigeon in the corner of the panel adds the precise correction as a speech-bubble text card ("Coo. Technically it's the *skin* that's striped"). It doubles as our accuracy device for policy (research/30). It needs one drawing, a grey blob with an eye and a beak, similar to `mascot.prop`. **Not built yet.**
- **The heckler animal** of the episode: the myth's animal gets one text-only line on a photo beat ("— the tiger, probably"). It is cheap, uses no voice, and stays optional.
- Rule: side characters never get the last joke in a Short; Meatball does.

## 6. Comedic structure

### Short (one myth, 20-26 s): Shorts v2 (research/38, accepted by the Mayor). Target 4-6 laughs, about one every 4-5 s
Rules: no quiz card and no countdown. Frame 0 is a moving full-frame photo. The claim is stated as a fact or as a correction. A new picture appears every 3 s or less, and each photo shows what its line says.
| Beat | Time | Picture | Content | Joke |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | 0-5 s | Full-bleed photo with slow push-in from frame 0, cut 2-3 times (crops) on stressed words; small deadpan Meatball on the photo | The claim as a fact or correction: "Tigers aren't just striped on the fur. Shave one, and the skin underneath is striped too." | Optional undercut ("That's not a myth. That's a cockroach.") |
| 2. Fact | 3-6 s | 2-3 photos and crops | One plain supporting sentence | A dry tag at the end ("…the head was basically optional") |
| 3. Turn | 3-4 s | The photo that shows the line (yawning tiger for "if one eats you"); Meatball switches to nervous | The food-anxiety turn | The main joke |
| 4. Cut-in | 3-4 s | **Meatball on white, the classic look**, nervous, with the Fork and the tink; the aside pops on the last word; 0.5 s hold | "I'm the one made of meat." / "I lose one bite, and they call it dinner." | Persona payoff |
| 5. Card | 3-5 s | The punch card on white; the text changes on the stressed word (STRIPES → MEATBALL ALL THE WAY DOWN) | A callback | Callback joke |
| 6. Loop | 2-3 s | The hook's first photo, zooming *out* to exactly where frame 0 starts | A line that runs into the first sentence ("I'm not saying you should try it. I'm just saying, —") | The loop is a joke too |
- Never more than one Fork and one sting per Short. The fact always comes before the joke.
- **Built:** `"hook": true` beats, `"shots"` (several pictures under one voice line, cut on a spoken word with `"at"`), `"crop"`, `"bleed"`, `"loop_say"`. tools/explainer.py `gate()` prints `GATE WARN` when a Short opens without a photo or holds one picture for more than 3 s.

### Long-form episode (12 myths, 6-9 min), target: 3-4 jokes per minute, as True Facts and Sam O'Nella do
- Cold open: one claim, plus a single-sentence persona line ("I'm Meatball. I narrate nature. Nature, frankly, keeps trying to eat me.").
- Per myth, keep the Short structure and add one aside.
- **One Threat Level card and up to 2 Fact-Check Pigeon corrections per episode.**
- A Casually Explained-style escalation across the episode: the Fork appears once in myth 2, twice by myth 8, and in the finale there are two forks [I].
- The outro gives the official list total plus one callback to an earlier episode's animal. No "smash like".

## 7. Sound: restrained
- **The voice carries the comedy.** Keep the quiet music bed (`music/quiz-bed.m4a`, ducked by the sidechain).
- **One signature sting: "tink"**, a fork tapping a plate. It is synthesized (no licensing) and mixed at half volume under the voice. It plays only when the Fork appears, at most once per Short. **Built:** `"sting": "tink"` (`SFX["tink"]` in tools/explainer.py).
- **Silence is the second instrument.** Before or after the main joke, `"hold": 0.4-0.6` leaves dead air, the True Facts pause.
- Do not bring back the buzzer/ding, whooshes, the bass hit, record scratches or laugh tracks.

## 10. Rules to keep it calm
1. The volume never rises. A joke is a word choice, a pause or a picture, never a louder line.
2. One sting per Short and one Fork per Short. If a script needs two, cut one.
3. Facts first, always straight; the joke comes second. No joke may change what the fact says.
4. Meatball is worried, not frantic: "I don't love it", never "AAAH".
5. The body stays still (research/21); the face and hands carry the acting.
6. The Short loop seam stays (research/23): the last frame is the first frame (the tail's zoom ends at 1.0 on the hook's first picture, and the hook's zoom starts there), the loop line runs into the opening sentence, and there is no music fade-in. The loop line carries the persona (`"loop_say"`).
7. Do not write jokes about real people, politics or crude body humour. We borrow the *devices* from Sam O'Nella and True Facts, not their edge or their lines.

## 11. Pilot and how to judge it
- The script is `scripts/pilot-showrunner.json` (Shorts v2): tiger and roach, the same facts as `originals-01`, plus four extra CC BY/CC0 tiger photos picked for what each line says (yawning tiger, mother and cubs, tiger from behind). It renders to `episodes/pilot-showrunner/short-{tiger,roach}.mp4`.
- New script fields: `pitch`, `short_beats` (default 3), and per beat `prop`, `aside`, `hold`, `sting`, `shots` (with `at` and `crop`), `bleed`, `hook`, and `loop_say`. Every one of them is optional; old scripts render as before.
- Scoring: post the pilot and the original of the same myth a week apart (Nolan decides). Compare stayed-to-watch (target 60%+ against ~40%, research/38 P1), APV (target over 100%, i.e. loops), likes per 1k (research/28: opinionated voices get 5-8%), and comments that mention "fork" or "not a snack". If stayed-to-watch doesn't move, the persona is not the problem; test the hook line next.

## 12. Format template: "You asked, Meatball answers" (Showrunner, 2026-10-24)
A viewer-question Short (20-26 s, Shorts v2 rules) that answers one real comment and credits the person who asked it. It turns comments into scripts, rewards commenting, and gives the next video a built-in reason to exist.

**Pick the question**
- It must come from a real comment on our videos. Copy it exactly into the script's `_note`, with the video ID and the date.
- It has to be answerable with one checked fact. Skip medical, pet-health or legal advice questions ("is my dog sick?"); reply to those in the comments instead.
- Prefer danger-to-you, gross biology or "wait, really?" questions (research/28).

**Credit rules**
- Use the commenter's first name *or* their public handle, exactly as shown on the comment. Never use a surname, location, avatar or any other detail.
- Credit people who look under 13, or whose comment mentions school or age, by handle only. Leave out anyone who seems upset or sensitive.
- If the comment is rude or off-topic, rewrite the question in neutral words and drop the credit ("A viewer asked").
- The credit goes in the on-screen text and the title line. It is never spoken as a joke at the person's expense.

**Beat sheet** (same fields as the pilot; `shot` is optional)
| Beat | Time | Picture | Line pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | 0-4 s | Full-bleed photo of the animal doing the thing; small text pill "@handle asked:" | The answer stated as a fact. Don't read the question out first ("Yes, an octopus can taste with its arms.") |
| 2. Credit + question | 3-4 s | Same photo, cropped closer; aside "(great question, @handle)" | "Ellie asked if that's real. It is." |
| 3. Fact | 3-6 s | 2-3 photos, each one matching its line | One plain supporting sentence |
| 4. Turn | 3-4 s | The photo that shows the line; Meatball nervous | The food-anxiety angle |
| 5. Cut-in | 3 s | Meatball on white, Fork + tink only if the line is about eating | Persona payoff |
| 6. Card + loop | 3-5 s | Text card "ASK ME YOURS 👇" plus the punch; tail returns to the hook photo | `loop_say` runs into the hook sentence |

**Script fields:** the hook beat carries `short_title` ("You Asked: Can an Octopus Taste With Its Arms? 🐙"), `comment` (pinned: "Ask me the next one 👇 I read them all."), `sources`, and the credit in an `aside` or `text`. Don't add new schema fields.

**Packaging:** start the title with "You Asked:", then the claim. Pin a reply on the *original* comment linking the Short ("Answered! 👉 [link]"); Nolan does this in Studio if the API can't.

**Limits:** at most one viewer-question Short in every three, so it stays a treat. Check every question with two sources, as for any Short (factdesk.md).

## v3 humor: Nolan loves it (10/24). Keep it in every Short.
- **Meatball suffers for the fact:** he tries, it goes wrong, and the true fact lands as the punchline.
- **Deadpan one-liners after each disaster** (from the v3 prototypes):
  - "Good news: I don't have a heart."
  - "I'm a pickle now."
  - "I evolved sauce."
  - "Best case? I become a tree."
- **Physical comedy with sound effects:**
  - kicked off-screen with X eyes
  - head puffs up after a sting
  - tumbles, trembles, peeks
- **Pacing:** a joke or escalation every 4-6 s, lines of 8 words or fewer, and a callback or twist at the end.
- Keep the voice calm and deadpan while the visuals go wild; that contrast is the joke.
