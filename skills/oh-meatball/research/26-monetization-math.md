# 26 — Monetization math: can Meatball Myths reach YPP by 2026-11-23? (The Accountant, 2026-10-24)

**Short answer: no, not ad revenue in 30 days.** The ad-revenue thresholds are 450-1,300x our current daily
numbers, and YouTube's review alone "typically" takes about a month. The realistic 30-day goal is to *qualify for
the lower tier*. Even that needs one or more Shorts to break out (100k+ views). The typical outcome is day 45-90.

Legend: [OFF] = official YouTube Help text (fetched 2026-10-24); [M] = our analytics (research/24); [I] = my inference.

## 1. The rules (official, 2026)
| Tier | Threshold [OFF] | Unlocks [OFF] |
|---|---|---|
| Lower ("expanded YPP") | "500 subscribers with 3 valid public uploads in the last 90 days, and 3,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months", **or** 500 subs + 3 uploads + 3 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days | Memberships, Super Chat/Stickers, Super Thanks, Jewels/gifts, Shopping. **No ad revenue.** The US is on the country list. |
| Full (ad revenue) | "1,000 subscribers with 4,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months", **or** 1,000 subs + "10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days" | Adds "revenue sharing from ads and YouTube Premium" |

- **Valid views [OFF]:** long-form counts "Qualified watch hours gained from long-form videos you've set public".
  Shorts count "Qualified views from Shorts you've set public that appear in the Shorts Feed".
  Private, unlisted and deleted videos do not count, and neither do ad-campaign views or feed Image Posts.
- **Shorts watch time does not count [OFF]:** "qualified watch hours from Shorts views in the Shorts Feed won't count towards the 4,000".
- **The view-counting change [OFF]:** "Beginning August 24, 2026, views are counted the moment a video starts to play". However, "YPP eligibility will still be based on 'qualified views'" and earnings on "engaged views".
  YouTube does not publish a definition of a qualified view. So the public view count may overstate our progress.
  - [I] Plan with the conservative number: our **engaged** views, 1,820 of 4,603 (40%).
- **Review [OFF]:** "Our automated systems and human reviewers will review your channel as a whole." A decision comes "typically in about 1 month".
  - After a rejection you can appeal "within 21 days" or "re-apply after a 30-day period".
  - After a second rejection the wait is "a 90-day period".
  - [I] A rejection therefore costs at least 30 days. Apply once, when the channel looks authored.
- **2027 note [OFF, Shorts monetization policy, answer/12504220]:** from Feb 1, 2027, earning any share of Shorts ad revenue requires "at least 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days". A partner that earns mostly from Shorts must keep a 10M pace.
- **Rejection risks for a faceless AI channel [OFF, answer/1311392].** This policy was renamed "inauthentic content" on July 15, 2025. Not allowed:
  - "Similar or repetitive content with low educational value, commentary, narratives, or minimal variation across videos"
  - "Videos where characters are put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome"
  - "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value"
  - "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates"

  Allowed: "Same intro and outro for your videos, but the bulk of your content is different".
- **Reused content [OFF]:** not allowed: "Content downloaded or copied from another online source without any substantive modifications". Our iNaturalist photos sit under narration and commentary, which is low risk [I].
- **AI disclosure [OFF, answer/14328491]:** disclosure is required for *realistic* altered or synthetic content.
  - A fully animated, unrealistic video does not need it.
  - Disclosure "alone doesn't reduce" reach or monetization.
  - Repeatedly failing to disclose can lead to "suspension from the YouTube Partner Program".
  - The page does not mention stock AI voices.
- **Our exposure [I]:** 4 uploads a day from one quiz template, in a stock TTS voice, with Meatball in "the same situation ... with the same outcome", is the exact pattern the policy describes.
  Human reviewers look at the channel "as a whole". Before applying, the recent uploads must show variation: the YOU-vs-MEATBALL score, the Pliny origin stories, the history episodes and the numbered series (research/22).

## 3. The math: daily numbers needed from today to day 30
| Path | Subs/day | Long-form views/day | Shorts qualified views/day | vs. today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** 1,000 subs + 4,000 h | 34 | **2,667** at 3.0 min AVD (3,200 at 2.5; 2,286 at 3.5) | n/a | long-form **~530x** (5 → 2,667); subs from 0 |
| **B** 1,000 subs + 10M Shorts | 34 | n/a | **333,000** (~111k per Short at 3/day) | **~500x** shown, **~1,300x** engaged |
| **C1** 500 subs + 3 uploads + 3M Shorts | 17 | n/a | **100,000** (~33k per Short) | **~150x** shown, **~385x** engaged |
| **C2** 500 subs + 3 uploads + 3,000 h | 17 | **2,000** at 3.0 min AVD | n/a | long-form **~400x** |

**Subscribers are not the binding constraint on the Shorts paths; views are.**
- Channels converting at a typical [I] 1-3 subs per 1k Shorts views would collect 3,000+ subs from 3M views.
- At today's rate (0 per 1k) we get nothing at any view count.
- To make 17 subs/day at 100k views/day takes only 0.17 subs per 1k. Below 0.5 per 1k, subs become the blocker.

**Why Path A is out [I]:** long-form has 2 suggested views in total [M].
- The only way into an episode is the Short's "Related video" link plus search.
- If 0.5-1% of Shorts viewers click through, 2,667 episode views a day need 270k-530k Shorts views a day. That is the Path B volume anyway.

## 4. Which path is fastest, and what to expect
- **Fastest: C1 (Shorts, lower tier), then B.** 82% of our traffic is the Shorts feed, and C1 needs the smallest multiple (150x).
- Shorts outliers are lumpy. One breakout does most of the work: research/23 found single-myth Shorts at 2.2M and 5.2M views on small channels.
- **Even a perfect 30 days does not put us in YPP by 11/23.** Review takes ~1 month, so we would have had to *apply* about today.
- So the honest target for 11/23 is: **eligible to apply for the lower tier, application submitted.**

| Scenario (30 days) | Shorts views | Subs | Long-form hours | Result on 11/23 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Typical (~60%) [I] | 30k-150k (current 1-5k/day, slow climb) | 20-150 | <50 | No tier. Lower tier is months away. |
| Good (~30%) [I] | 0.5M-1.5M (2-4 Shorts at 100k-500k) | 300-1,000 | 50-300 | Lower tier at day ~45-75 (3M inside 90 days) |
| Great (~5-10%) [I] | 3M+ (one 2M+ breakout + follow-ups) | 1,000+ | 300+ | Eligible for the lower tier ~day 25-30; accepted ~day 55-60 if review passes |
| Ad tier in 30 days | 10M | 1,000 | — | **<1%.** Plan on ~90 days, and only if breakouts repeat |

- A rising ramp shows the gap: 2k/day in week 1, 10k in week 2, 40k in week 3, then 100k for the last 9 days, totals only **1.26M**.
  Even that aggressive ramp puts the lower tier at about day 50, not day 30.
- **Is the lower tier reachable sooner?** Yes. It is the only realistic first milestone, at 3.3x fewer views and half the subs of Path B.
  - It pays fan funding only, which is near $0 at our size.
  - Its real value is a passed human review. It proves the channel clears the inauthentic-content check before we stake the ad-tier application on it.
- TikTok already pays (Creator Rewards); YouTube does not. Do not let the YPP push weaken the TikTok output [I].

## 7. What the Accountant recommends
1. Set the goal honestly: **"Lower tier eligible and applied by about 12/15. Ad tier by about Q1 2027 via Path B."** Do not promise YPP by 11/23.
2. Put the effort into Metric 3 (the open) and Metric 2 (a reason to subscribe). Adding volume to a 40% open multiplies a weak base.
3. Cut long-form cost, or treat it only as policy proof that the channel is authored, until Related-video clicks deliver more than 20 views per episode. It contributes ~0 to every path today.
4. Before applying, audit the last 30 uploads against the four inauthentic-content lines above. One rejection costs 30+ days, which is more than our whole window.
