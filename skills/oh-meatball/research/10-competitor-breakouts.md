## 0. Headline findings

1. **The breakouts live on Instagram Reels and TikTok, not on YouTube Shorts.**
   - Episodic animal-soap outliers of 1M to 39M views are almost all Reels or TikToks **[M]**.
   - On YouTube Shorts, an "AI cat drama part 2" search found no episodic AI cat soap above about 30k views. Every real Shorts breakout was a one-off story **[M]**.
2. **Parts are longer than ours.** Watched breakouts ran 47 to 131 s, with 15 to 29 shots each **[W]**. Our parts are 16 to 32 s.
3. **The biggest hits are complete mini-arcs.** Each one runs setup, then suffering, then rescue or reversal, then an emotional payoff. A "Part N" label is optional. The 38.9M and 1.5M outliers had no part label at all **[W]**.
4. **Our channel uploaded Part 1 twice.** `iAKwF9YyCpc` and `L5pOwnR3ieE` went up 2 minutes apart with the same title **[M]**. Fix this first (see "Do this now").

## 3. The pattern, as testable rules, ranked by evidence

### A. Proven by multiple outliers (at least 4 independent accounts)

1. **Frame 0 is the inciting disaster, already happening.**
   - Examples: a sack falls on Mom, she cuts off her hair, Mom walks out, the husband leaves with the rich pig, an axe swing, a pregnancy test, "What happened to my children?" spoken at 0 s.
   - None opens on scenery or an establishing shot. Evidence: 9 of 9 hooks reviewed **[W/M]**.
2. **The cast is recurring, named, and branded on screen.**
   - Names are printed on shirts (BAILY / BELLA), shown on crowd signs (LOVE MILO), and used as a hashtag (#BailyAndBella, #MiloAndMimi). The profile picture is the duo.
   - This is the clearest follower-conversion lever: @bailybella26 gained 284K followers from 102 posts. It is consistent with @itswofi in research 08 **[M]**.
3. **Each post holds a complete arc of 45–130 s with 15–29 cuts (one cut every 2–4 s).**
   - Evidence: 7 of 7 narrative watches **[W]**; research 08 found Reels at 60–90 s and TikTok at 90–500 s **[M]**.
   - The YouTube Shorts story winners run 26–53 s (AniDaily 53, Snicker Paws 44, Cozy Paws 26, MercyPulse 39) **[M]**.
4. **The tropes are a fixed menu of class and family melodrama.**
   - Pregnant wife abandoned for a rich rival.
   - Poor single mother sacrifices herself.
   - Cheating partner.
   - A "rescue / reversal / 3 years later" glow-up.
   - Twins: favored vs. outcast.
   - Evidence: about 12 accounts **[M]**.
5. **Long compilations sit beside the parts.**
   - TikTok winners post 2–8 minute multi-part stitches (meow.meows08 499 s, sharkshortdrama 288 s, meow.reels12 with 2 stories).
   - YouTube winners post long compilations (Cozy Paws averages about 2 h per long video; On My Path posts 7–12 minute episode compilations).
   - Evidence: 5+ accounts **[M]**.
6. **Titles and captions follow "[Name or role] [did X]… Then [Y] 🥹/😭".** Examples:
   - "He Was Too Small to Save Mom Alone… Then Baily Came"
   - "Hungry Little Mimi Had Nothing… Milo Did Everything for Her"
   - "The Storm Kept Blowing the Door Open… He Never Left"
   - The ellipsis gap plus an emotion emoji plus a character hashtag appears in 5+ cases **[M]**.

### B. Moderate evidence (2–3 accounts)

7. **When a cliffhanger is used, a character states the CTA out loud and the cut lands one beat before the reveal.** Examples: "Like and comment for part two", "comment 'Love Mimi'", text "part2 ?". Seen in 3 of 7 watches **[W]**.
8. **Volume is daily or more.** @fruits.cantalk says "new episodes daily" (170 posts). Dream Ai Story posted 20 Shorts in 7 days, and RishtonXDrama about 6 per week; both are young breakout channels **[M]**. Exact winning frequency is unproven **[I]**.
9. **Music-only or "meow-song" versions travel globally.** Examples: @bailybella26, @markmogerz12, @catvideos024, @cat70050, @catmagicxv (a "mew mew" song). These have no language barrier **[M]**. Dialogue versions also win (fruits.cantalk, aantics46, animation.oura), so neither is required.
10. **Reposting across platforms is normal.** @animation.oura's 7.3M reel carries another creator's watermark (@halastory08) **[W]**.
    - So: watermark our handle on every frame, and post the originals first on IG and TikTok ourselves.

### C. Anecdotal or unproven

- **Last frame looping into the first:** not observed in any narrative breakout. Use it only for the 16–20 s YouTube cuts.
- **Small accounts can spike:** @dramatoon27 hit 5M views at 4.2K followers, which suggests the Reels algorithm gives new accounts a fair shot **[I]**.
- **Monetization signals:** @bailybella26 links an AI-app affiliate (Jelly AI) and lists a business email **[M]**.
- **Episode-number suffixes** ("-72", "Ai 20") appear on industrial accounts, but there is no evidence they matter.

## 4. Implications for Oh Meatball

- **Platform:** our 16–32 s parts suit YouTube Shorts, but the proven breakout surface for this niche is IG Reels and TikTok at 45–130 s. Run both:
  - short cliffhanger parts for YouTube;
  - stitched "full episode" cuts (Parts 1–3, about 60 s) for Reels and TikTok.
- **Wedding-crash premise:** "If anyone objects…" plus Smokey plus Duchess's secret already matches trope #4. Lean into class and secrecy (Duchess rich or hiding a pregnancy; Smokey's past) rather than slapstick.
- **First frame:** it must show the objection or crash in progress with the line already being spoken. No establishing shot of the church.
