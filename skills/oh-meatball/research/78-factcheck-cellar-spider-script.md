# 78. Fact-check: "Never Kill the Spider in Your Corner. It's Hunting Bigger Spiders" (cellar spider long-form), 2026-11-10

Independent second-reviewer check of `scripts/longform-dont-squish-cellar-spider.outline.json` (132 narration lines, their cards and the `vis` notes that put a claim on screen) against fact pack `research/76-cellar-spider-factpack.md` and, for the load-bearing lines, against the live sources. Read-only: no script or other file was edited, nothing was committed.

Line numbers below count `say` lines from 1 to 132 in script order (segment by segment).

## 0. Verdict

- **OK: 107 lines. SOFTEN: 25 lines (4 of them card or picture only). CUT: 0.**
- No line touches the "Do NOT say" table head-on (no "eats recluses", no bug-per-month number, no "too short to bite", no MythBusters "proved it"). The problems are smaller overreaches plus three lines that misread their source.

The three most serious problems:

1. **"Can it hurt you? Nobody has ever shown that it can." (line 71) and "Can she hurt you? Nobody has ever shown it." (line 126).** Wrong. The 2019 paper the script itself cites documents bites: a mild sting, a small welt that "blackened and eventually opened into a 1 mm diameter wound", and a knuckle that "was sore and slightly inflamed for several days". The script's own line 67 says a bite gave a sting. The honest answer is "barely, and mildly", not "never shown".
2. **The Hampshire shed numbers (lines 18 and 19, plus the pie chart).** The factsheet says the six were "other Daddy-longlegs spiders" (cellar spiders eating cellar spiders), and "the rest were unidentifiable spider remains". So all 102 leftovers were spiders, not "two out of every three". The pack's F27 note ("six more were other spiders") passed on the misreading.
3. **The whirling study (lines 102 and 103, plus the "Carol yawns" picture).** The abstract says *long* whirling was "hardly ever" shown to the eight other families, and that *short* whirling helps survival "in the presence of all sorts of predatory spiders". "Barely got a reaction" is wrong. "Scientists think the blur makes her hard for a hunter to lock onto" turns the authors' "may", about jumping spiders only, into a general statement.

Also important: line 92 ("First time the shoe is ahead") contradicts the meter, which starts at 30 and sits at 34 after line 49. Line 90 drops "some" from the Finnish study.

## 3. Could not verify

- **Line 39 / F33 (Brazil lab).** The SciELO abstract returned 403 to curl. I read it only through the fetch tool's summarizer, which quoted "The predation on L.intermedia was greater despite P.phalangioides be until five times lighter", that insects were preferred, and "Probably only the presence of P.phalangioides in the antropic environment is not enough". The wording at lines 39 and 40 matches. Before putting "5x" on screen, have someone open the abstract in a browser. Safe fallback: "In a lab in Brazil, cellar spiders killed recluse spiders much heavier than they were." Card: MUCH HEAVIER.
- **Line 35 (aggressive mimicry) and line 37 (doorbell gag).** Attested second-hand (Japyassu & Macagnan citing Jackson & Brassington 1987; Missouri for "some species"). The 1987 paper was not opened. The current wording ("Scientists call it", "Some cellar spiders") is safe; keep it.
- **Line 8 "Body about a quarter inch".** Penn State gives 7 to 8 mm for females (0.28 to 0.31 in); BAS gives 8 to 10 mm (to 0.39 in). "About a quarter inch" rounds down slightly. Left as OK because Clemson gives 1/4 to 5/16 inch, but "about a third of an inch" would be more exact for British spiders. No change required.
- **Line 66 "A TV show tried it for real."** The paper says the bites happened "from aggression" among aggregated males, so it was not a planned bite test. Acceptable for narration, but if you want it exact: "On a TV show, a cellar spider bit a person on camera."

## 4. Titles

1. **"Never Kill the Spider in Your Corner. It's Hunting Bigger Spiders"**: SOFTEN. "It's Hunting Bigger Spiders" is supported (BAS: much heavier large house spiders; Missouri: "Often, they capture prey much larger than themselves"). "Never Kill" is absolute. The video's own advice section cites Iowa State and Kentucky, whose vacuuming removes the spiders too, and UC IPM, which says "Squash spiders or capture them in a jar". Also, "the spider in your corner" is not always a cellar spider. Suggested: "Don't Kill the Skinny Spider in Your Corner. It Eats Bigger Spiders".
2. **"Don't Squish the Skinny Spider in Your Corner (Here's What It Eats)"**: OK. The diet chapter delivers (flying insects, woodlice, house spiders, giant house spiders, web owners). "Skinny" also narrows it to the cellar spider. The safest of the three.
3. **"The Spider in Your Ceiling Corner Is Hunting Something Bigger"**: OK with a note. Supported by Missouri ("Often ... much larger") and BAS. Like title 1, it assumes the viewer's spider is a cellar spider; "The Skinny Spider in Your Ceiling Corner..." would close that gap.
