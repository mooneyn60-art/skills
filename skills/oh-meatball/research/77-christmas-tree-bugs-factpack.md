# 77. Christmas tree bugs: verified fact pack (Meatball Myths long-form)

Compiled 2026-11-10. Every URL below was actually opened with WebFetch unless marked UNVERIFIED. Quotes were relayed through the fetch tool's page summarizer, so a checker should re-read the exact wording on the live page for any line that will be quoted on screen. Where the tool said it could not give verbatim text, the Note column says "paraphrase relayed" and confidence is capped at medium.

## 0. Read this first

- SOLID: the cast and the advice (inspect, shake, clip egg cases and put them outside, vacuum not squash, no aerosol spray) come from Penn State, Illinois, NC State, Iowa State, Missouri. Nearly every creature is described as a nuisance that cannot live long in a dry heated house.
- SHAKY: the "25,000 bugs" number. The only primary trace is a 2012 University of Bergen web article quoting Jordal ("as many as 25,000 ... in some of the trees"), about Norwegian trees, with no method given. The original study was NOT found.
- SHAKY: "1 tree in 100,000" is NC State's own hedged estimate ("Perhaps"), repeated by Illinois. It is not a measured rate.
- NOT FOUND: any extension figure for how long to leave a tree in a garage; any NCTA primary statement (Snopes returned 402); a peer-reviewed paper on Christmas tree arthropods; Ohio State, Cornell, Oregon State and Michigan State pages on this topic (not located). Pennsylvania Dept of Agriculture page was not opened (only quoted via Fox News).
- CONFLICT: sources disagree on aerosol sprays (NC State allows with lights unplugged; Penn State, Illinois, Iowa State say do not) and on whether Cinara can reproduce indoors (Penn State and NC State yes; Iowa State says generic tree pests will not live long enough to multiply).

## 3. Do NOT say

- **"A Christmas tree holds up to 25,000 bugs."** Source: one 2012 Bergen article, "as many as 25,000 ... in some of the trees", Norwegian trees, no method found, counts insects plus spiders and other crawlers. Iowa State says an infestation runs from a few to several hundred. Safe: "A Norwegian museum biologist once reported as many as 25,000 tiny creatures in some trees; US extension services describe a few to several hundred."
- **"1 in 100,000 trees has bugs."** It is NC State's "perhaps" estimate about three specific pests. Safe: "NC State estimates that perhaps one tree in 100,000 carries one of these pests."
- **"Every mantis case holds 200 to 400 eggs."** Illinois says 200 to 400 per female in the fall; Missouri says about 100 per mass; Mississippi State says several hundred; NC State says 50 to 600. I could not confirm that one female makes several cases. Safe: "from around a hundred to several hundred eggs, depending on the species and source."
- **"A tree is covered in egg cases" or "this happens to most trees."** No source supports it; NCTA's "very rare" quote is UNVERIFIED.
- **"Hatch within days."** Only "after several weeks" (Penn State) was found. Do not give days.
- **"Leave the tree in the garage for X hours/days."** No extension figure found.
- **"Aerosol spray is fine if you unplug the lights."** Only NC State's grower page says it; Penn State, Illinois and Iowa State say no.
- **"Bark beetles will eat your furniture/house."** Penn State: they cannot develop in dry wood.
- **"Aphids bite" or "are ticks."** They do not bite (NC State); real ticks are only "a few instances".
- **"The bugs will infest your houseplants."** Illinois: tree aphids target specific species and will not harm houseplants.
- **"Fake trees are bug-free" or any fake-tree claim.** No extension source found.
- **"Hose the tree" as universal advice.** NC State suggests it; Jordal advised against washing. Say "some extensions suggest".
- **Any "bugs hatch and take over the house" language.** Every extension source says they die quickly indoors (F20, F21).
- **"Cinara are the largest aphids in the world."** Only a relayed paraphrase; use NC State's "among the largest aphids we have here".
