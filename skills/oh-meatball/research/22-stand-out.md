# 22 — How Meatball Myths stands out (researched 2026-10-23)

**Brief.** The owner prefers the original calm look: white background, a photo panel, a big Meatball, and simple text cards. This report therefore ranks ways to stand out through **writing, the character, recurring bits and formats**, not through more effects. I checked that preference against what the outliers actually do (section 1). It mostly holds, with one caveat about pacing.

**Legend.** [M] = measured here (yt-dlp flat metadata, data/rivals.csv, thumbnails viewed). [OFF] = official platform page. [3P] = third-party source. [I] = my inference. [UNVERIFIED] = not confirmed.

## 1. Findings

### 1a. What the 3x+ outliers in data/rivals.csv have in common [M]
I recomputed each rival's newest-20 median and kept videos at 3x or more.
- **Topic and promise drive the outliers, not production.**
  - Myths-creature "7 EXTINCT Animals That Might Still Be ALIVE" (49.6x); Mysterious-Fact "Mythical Sea Creatures That Really Existed!" (41.5x) and its Part 2 (17x).
  - Lying Pie "40 Cat Myths That Need to Finally Die" (16.3x); Quiz Madness "How Well Do You Know Animals? 50 Animal Trivia Questions" (18.8x); What Gives? "Worst Animal Mating - Seals" (10.7x, a Short).
- **Series continuity is everywhere.**
  - Every What Gives? Short in its newest 25 belongs to a numbered mini-series: "Stupid Animal Names #3", "Weirdest Laws In USA #5", "Dumbest Deaths #3", "Worst Mating Ritual - X".
  - Its long-form are numbered too ("Part 10").
  - Mysterious-Fact's outliers are Parts 2, 4 and 5 plus a "Full Series" compilation.
- **Each rival has one signature, and it is a format rule, not an effect.**
  - Lying Pie's channel description: "Every video delivers exactly 40 facts — our signature format."
  - Oddly Factual titles every video "42 ...".
  - What Gives? carries one voice everywhere, down to its About text: "Instead of watching the videos, you are reading this description. But what gives? :/".
  - Quiz Madness sells a human host: "researched, written, designed, and voiced by me".
- **Thumbnails (I viewed 8) [M].**
  - **What Gives?**
    - Look: a white background, a crude stick-figure face reacting in the top-left corner, drawn (not photographed) animals, and 2-3 coloured words with a series number ("Weird ANIMAL FACTS #6").
    - Joke device: a hand-drawn strikethrough ("ANIMAL ~~FACTS~~ lies #2").
    - Its Short (XQia6nrPKZY) is a plain white card with one drawing and a small lowercase caption ("not 1, but 4 times").
  - **Others:** Lying Pie uses a parchment naturalist sketch with a "MYTH" stamp; Myths-creature and Mysterious-Fact use dramatic AI-rendered creatures with EXTINCT→ALIVE arrows; Quiz Madness uses two A/B photo tiles.
  - The take-away: **the calmest-looking channel (What Gives?) is the biggest** (109k subscribers, a long-form median of 129k views) [M].
- **Caveat on "restraint" [M + I].** research/21 timed What Gives? at a shot change every 1.5-3 s (95% hard cuts), with mouth flaps and a pose swap every 1.5-2.8 s.
  - Their *look* is calm; their *rhythm* is not. The calm look is supported if each line still brings a new card, drawing or photo. Dropping shakes, stamps and the progress bar is fine; holding one card for 8 s is not.

### 1b. Where every rival is the same, and where the space is open [M + I]
| Axis | What all 7 rivals do | Open space |
|---|---|---|
| Host | A narrator with no on-screen identity (What Gives? has a face but no name or story) | A **named host with a personality and a stake** in the quiz |
| Voice | One narrator, no other characters | **A second character** (an antagonist) and dialogue |
| Humour | Joke captions on facts | **Running gags and callbacks** across episodes |
| Format | Narrated lists; nobody plays a game with the viewer (research/16) | **The host plays too**, with a score, and loses |
| Myth angle | "Is it true?" | **"Who started this lie?"**: the myth's origin as the punchline |
| Interactivity | "Comment below" at most | Native quiz stickers, answer keys, viewer-myth replies |

### 1c. Character-led, low-fi channels that grew [M for subscribers and views; dates UNVERIFIED]
None of these is a verified 2025-26 breakout. Web searches found no documented 2025-26 case study of a faceless animal-facts breakout, only vendor blogs. These are the closest evidence of what makes a simple-looking channel beloved.
- **Natural Habitat Shorts** (6.24M subscribers) dubs real animal clips, and its hook is **named recurring characters**.
  - Compilations: "Kevin's Tales of Woe" (406k), "Batrick Compilation" (878k), "Lisa's best moments" (225k, made "in honor of our new Lisa merch").
- **Casually Explained** (4.22M) is a deadpan stick figure. Its Shorts are a recurring-character series, "The Cyclist™" (35k-227k each).
- **Sam O'Nella Academy** (4.81M): crude stick figures and deadpan narration.
  - "Pliny Explains it All: The Historia Naturalis Abridged" has 9.4M views, and its sequel has 3.4M.
  - The joke: an ancient author's confidently wrong animal facts. That is exactly our niche.
- **TierZoo** (3.95M) uses one framing metaphor everywhere (animals as video-game builds): "2026 PATCH NOTES JUST DROPPED" (760k).
- **Animals Recapped** (326k) gives the host an attitude: "Why Wolverines Are the Most Unhinged Predators on Earth" (892k).
- Pattern [I]: each one has **one ownable device**: a named character, a nemesis, a metaphor or a verdict word. None relies on effects.

### 1e. Cheap vs beloved, and monetization risk
- **Policy text** [OFF] (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392):
  - Not allowed: "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value" and "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates".
  - Allowed: the "same intro and outro ... but the bulk of your content is different".
  - The page never mentions AI voices.
- **What third parties claim** [3P, unverified]: enforcement looks at patterns channel-wide, and the risk is "automated, interchangeable, and repetitive" output. https://outlierkit.com/resources/faceless-youtube-channel-demonetized/, https://gulfnews.com/technology/youtube-updates-monetisation-policies-ai-and-repetitive-content-ban-begins-july-15-1.500192660
- **Our exposure [I]:**
  - We ship 7 uploads a day cut from one template, in a stock voice (en-US-AndrewMultilingualNeural, widely used on faceless channels [UNVERIFIED]). What protects us is per-video writing only this channel could produce: opinions, callbacks, a cast, origins.
- **Why AI voices feel cheap [I, from 1c]:** they have no point of view, every video sounds the same, and nobody on screen has a relationship with anyone else.
- **What makes simple channels beloved [I]:** the beloved ones are crude to look at but specific in voice: deadpan opinions, named characters, recurring bits that reward returning viewers.

## 3. Top 8 differentiation ideas (ranked by expected impact ÷ build effort)

### 1. Meatball plays too, and is often wrong ("YOU vs MEATBALL")
- **What.** Before each reveal, Meatball commits to a guess out loud ("Easy. FALSE."). He is confidently wrong about a third of the time. A running tally (YOU vs MEATBALL) shows at each reveal, and the pinned comment asks "Did you beat Meatball?"
- **Why.** No rival host plays the game (table 1b). A lovable loser is the most proven character device in this list: "Kevin's Tales of Woe" (406k) and The Cyclist™. A host who loses also gives viewers a target score, which turns "comment your score" into bragging rights [I].
- **Build.**
  - Add a beat field `"meatball_guess": "TRUE"|"FALSE"`.
  - In compose() (tools/explainer.py), draw a small speech bubble during the countdown and "MEATBALL 4 · YOU ?" on the reveal card.
  - Add a pose `"smug"` before the reveal and `"defeated"` after a wrong guess (tools/mascot.py, one new mouth/brow combination each).
  - Writer rule: wrong on 3-4 of 12, and wrong on the myth everyone gets wrong.
- **Measure.** Comments per 1k views and the share of comments containing a score, compared with eps 3-5. Long-form APV.

### 2. Numbered Shorts mini-series with fixed title templates
- **What.** Every Short belongs to a numbered mini-series with a fixed title template:
  - "Animal Lie #14: Goldfish Memory"
  - "Worst Animal Dad #3"
  - "Meatball Got It Wrong #2"
- **Why.**
  - Every What Gives? Short is a numbered mini-series [M], and its "Worst Animal Mating" run hit 10.7x [M].
  - YouTube links consecutive entries of a repeated format for viewers who liked an earlier one ([3P], Hoyos, research/20).
  - The numbers also build a back catalogue that viewers want to complete [I].
- **Build.**
  - Add `"series"` and `"n"` to each Short spec in tools/episode.py, with a counter per series in data/series.csv.
  - Draw a small "ANIMAL LIE #14" header in compose() (todo in research/21).
  - Have tools/meta.py build the title from the template.
- **Measure.** Views at 48 h for numbered vs unnumbered Shorts, and subscribers gained per 1k views.

### 3. "Who started this lie?" origin beat, with Pliny as the recurring culprit
- **What.** Every myth's explanation ends by naming its origin. For example:
  - Lemmings: Disney's 1958 film *White Wilderness* staged the cliff scene (well documented).
  - Bears licking shapeless cubs into shape, elephants hating mice and ostriches thinking they are hidden: Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* [verify each line against the text before scripting].
  - Pliny becomes a recurring stick-figure antagonist (beard, toga, a scroll) with his own voice, who states his myth smugly before Meatball debunks it.
- **Why.**
  - Rivals answer "is it true?" but never "who lied?" (table 1b).
  - "Lied to you" titles are measured Shorts winners (research/16: "You've Been Lied To About Rats" 750k; Lion King 653x).
  - Pliny's wrong animal facts already carried 9.4M + 3.4M views for Sam O'Nella [M]. So the joke works with this audience, but we must not copy his treatment: use our own lines, and use Pliny only as a recurring guest.
  - A second voice adds narrative and commentary, the very thing the policy says separates a channel from "templated" content [OFF].
  - It also links straight into the "Meatball Explains" history series.
- **Build.**
  - Add a beat field `"speaker": "pliny"`. voice() in tools/explainer.py maps it to a second edge-tts voice (for example en-GB-RyanNeural with pitch -10Hz; check which voices are available).
  - Draw the Pliny figure in mascot.py: a beard arc plus a toga triangle on the existing body.
  - Add an `"origin"` field per myth and add "Started by:" to the pinned answer key.
- **Measure.** Long-form APV and the retention dip at each origin beat (Studio retention graph). Also count comments that mention Pliny.

### 4. One signature verdict word and a "how wrong" meter
- **What.** Replace the generic TRUE/FALSE call with a word the channel owns: FALSE becomes "**RAW.**" and TRUE becomes "**FULLY COOKED.**" (the meatball pun). Every FALSE also gets a 1-5 meatball "how wrong is it" rating, said in one deadpan line.
- **Why.**
  - Each rival owns one signature: Lying Pie's 40, Oddly Factual's 42, What Gives?'s "but what gives?", TierZoo's game vocabulary [M].
  - A verdict word is repeatable in comments ("RAW") and recognisable with the sound on [I].
  - The meter gives viewers something to argue about ("that's a 5, not a 3") [I].
- **Build.**
  - A writer rule: reveal lines open with the verdict word.
  - A text card with 1-5 meatball icons, drawn in compose(); the icon is the existing head from mascot._head scaled down.
  - Add `"wrongness": 1-5` to the reveal beat.
  - If the word confuses viewers, show TRUE/FALSE small underneath.
- **Measure.** Count comments using "raw" or "cooked" (`vidiq_video_comments`). Test the word in 3 Shorts against 3 without it, scored on stayed-to-watch.

### 5. Native TRUE/FALSE quiz sticker, plus viewer-myth replies
- **What.**
  - Every Short gets YouTube's quiz sticker (two options: TRUE / FALSE) over the claim.
  - Each week one Short answers a viewer's myth, with their comment shown on screen. On TikTok it is posted as a reply-to-comment video.
  - The CTA becomes "Comment a myth you were taught. Meatball will rule on it."
- **Why.** The sticker's two options match our format exactly [OFF], and TikTok replies post as new videos linked to the comment [OFF]. Comments seed TikTok recommendations [OFF]. Showing viewers' names rewards commenting [I]. The reach effect of stickers is UNVERIFIED.
- **Build.**
  - Sticker: a Nolan task at upload in the YouTube app (Showrunner adds it to OPERATIONS.md). Replies: a beat field `"comment": {"user": "@x", "text": "..."}` renders a white comment-bubble card in compose() as the first frame.
  - The Inbox role picks one myth a week.
- **Measure.** Comments per 1k views, quiz-sticker taps (Studio), and TikTok views of replies compared with normal posts.

### 6. Long-form packaging the rivals don't use
- **What.** Three title angles:
  - **Blame:** "The Man Who Invented 12 Animal Myths (and Got Away With It)"; "Who Lied to You About These 12 Animals?"
  - **Host fallibility:** "I Got 5 of These Animal Myths WRONG"
  - **Rated:** "Animal Myths Ranked From Slightly Wrong to Completely RAW"
  - The thumbnail stays in our calm style but owns two elements: a white background with **Meatball's big reacting head** plus **one** real photo, and a red marker strike-through or a "RAW" label with a series number.
- **Why.**
  - Rivals' titles are "N myths you believe / that really existed" (research/16).
  - The proven thumbnail grammar is white background + stick-figure face + series number + strikethrough (What Gives?, [M]).
  - The real photo is our point of difference from drawn rivals.
  - The best performers still come from the proven topics, so put the angle on top of a backlog topic. Do not replace the topic [M, 1a].
- **Build.**
  - A "calm" layout preset in tools/thumb3.py: white canvas, mascot head at about 45% height, one photo tile, a red strike line, a "#n" badge.
  - tools/meta.py gets title-angle templates.
  - Nolan runs Test & Compare (Studio-only).
- **Measure.** Impressions CTR against eps 1-5 and the Test & Compare winner. Keep the 30 s retention target at 70% or more.

### 7. Meatball acts out the myth (one stick-figure gag card per myth)
- **What.** Each myth gets one card where Meatball physically acts out the myth on white:
  - his head in a sand pile (ostrich);
  - holding a dice-shaped poop (wombat);
  - fleeing a tiny mouse (elephant).
  - It replaces a text card, not a photo.
- **Why.**
  - What Gives? jokes are drawn gags on white [M].
  - Casually Explained and Sam O'Nella prove crude stick-figure acting carries comedy [M].
  - It adds a new visual event every few seconds without any effects (section 2).
  - Each re-enactment is unique per video, which is the opposite of a "generic template" [OFF policy text].
- **Build.**
  - A `"prop"` field on text beats; mascot.draw() gets a small PROPS dict of PIL shape functions (sand mound, cube, mouse, hat), about 20 lines each.
  - Start with 6 reusable props and add 1-2 per episode.
- **Measure.** The retention graph at gag cards against plain text cards (Studio, long-form), and Shorts APV.

### 8. Cross-episode lore: the "Myth Graveyard" counter and callbacks
- **What.** A running total across the channel ("Myths sent to the graveyard: 61"), shown on the cold-open card and in every description. Each episode includes one callback to an earlier myth or to Meatball's earlier loss ("Still not over the goldfish thing").
- **Why.**
  - Recurring characters and callbacks are what Natural Habitat Shorts monetises (character compilations, merch) [M].
  - Callbacks reward returning viewers, and returning viewers are what a channel building toward YPP needs [I].
  - It is also cheap evidence of an authored series, not "interchangeable" uploads [3P].
- **Build.**
  - data/graveyard.csv (myth, verdict, episode, date); tools/meta.py adds the count to descriptions.
  - The Writer reads the CSV and must include one callback per episode.
- **Measure.** The returning-viewers share (YouTube Analytics), subscribers per 1k views, and comments that reference earlier episodes.

## 4. Order of work, and what not to do
- **Week 1:** ideas 1, 2, 4 (script and text-card changes) plus the calm-look experiment. **Week 2:** 3 and 7 (new drawings, second voice). **Ongoing:** 5, 6, 8.
- Every change gets an experiment row in data/experiments.csv: one variable at a time, scored after 3 videos.
- **Don't** copy Sam O'Nella's Pliny jokes or What Gives?'s drawn style 1:1 (borrow the device, not the content); don't let the cast delay myth #1 past 15 s; don't add effects to rescue a flat line, cut the line.
