# Fact pack: why your dog stares at you (slug dog-stare, series 69)

Research date: 2026-11-12. Searched phrase: "why does my dog stare at me" (Google Trends, YouTube search, US, 3 months to 2026-11-12: 74 against "why does my dog lick me" 100; value comes from the brief, not re-measured here).

## 2. Three hook facts
1. DOGS1 + DOGS3: in one study, after a 30-minute reunion, dogs' gazing raised oxytocin in their owners' urine; wolves' did not. (Number: 30 minutes, source PMC4663257. Pair count not confirmed: leave it out.)
2. DOGS7: many dogs stare because they are waiting for a cue (the leash, a meal). Source AKC, no number.
3. DOGS8: a hard stare with unblinking eyes and a stiff posture is a warning; AKC, Cornell and Texas A&M agree.

## 3. Do not say
| Tempting line | Why not |
|---|---|
| "Your dog stares because it loves you." | A feeling claim. The study measured urine oxytocin, not love. |
| "The study proves love" or "proves a bond". | The authors say "may have supported" and a commentary calls the evolution step premature. |
| "All dogs do this." | One study; a pair count is unconfirmed; the long-gaze pairs are the ones that showed the rise. |
| "Staring makes oxytocin go up." (cause) | The rise went with long gazes (a correlation within the study). The abstract does say gazing increased owner oxytocin, but the nasal-oxytocin arm is the causal test; keep "in one study" and "went with". |
| "Wolves can't bond with people." | The result is about owner oxytocin after a 30-minute reunion with tamed wolves. |
| Any pair count (21, 30) or wolf count (11) | Not confirmed from a primary page. |
| "Stare back and win." / "Stare down a strange dog." | AKC says never stare down strange dogs. Never tell a viewer to do it. |
| "A staring dog is dangerous." | Only a hard stare with unblinking eyes and a stiff body. Most staring is waiting or asking. |
| "Your dog is begging, manipulating or being rude." | A motive claim. |

## 4. Shortened-claim traps
- "Dog stares raise oxytocin" drops "in one study", "in owners' urine", "long gazes" and "after a 30-minute reunion".
- "Staring is a warning" is false for most staring. Keep "hard stare, unblinking, stiff body".
- "Dogs stare for love" drops that most pages say waiting or asking comes first.
- "Wolves did not" without "tamed wolves, owner oxytocin" reads as a claim about all wolves.
- "Back away" without the trigger turns into advice for every dog that looks at you.

## 5. Disagreements between sources
- Pair count: a search summary said 30 owners (24 female, 6 male); Time was said to report 21 pairs. Neither opened. Unresolved.
- Wolves: "tamed" (Frontiers commentary) against "hand-raised" (National Geographic, not opened).
- Conclusion: authors say the loop "may have supported the coevolution"; Fiset and Plourde say that is premature.
- Eye contact: AKC and Texas A&M say avoid eye contact with an aggressive-looking dog. This is about a threatening dog, not your own relaxed dog looking at you.
