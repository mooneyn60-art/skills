## 0. Read this first

- SOLID: Li et al. 2005, PLoS Genetics 1(1):e3 (Monell Chemical Senses Center). Full text opened raw from PMC (CC BY). The main fact is confirmed from the paper today: the cat Tas1r2 gene has a 247-base pair microdeletion and stop codons, there was no detectable expression, so cats "may be unable" and "most likely" cannot detect sweet-tasting compounds. The paper's own hedge ("may", "most likely", "likely necessary") is what the video must keep.
- SOLID, independent: Zhao et al. 2010 (Mol Biol Evol) and Sandau et al. 2015 (BMC Neurosci), abstracts opened raw. Both list the cat Tas1r2 as a lost gene or pseudogene. Two later papers, different teams, same finding.
- SOLID for "what cats taste instead": McGrane et al. 2023 (Chem Senses, abstract raw): umami is proposed as the main appetitive taste. Funded and written by Mars Petcare staff, which is stated in the paper.
- SHAKY: why a cat begs at cake. No paper I opened says why. Cake has fat, milk and eggs, but I found no primary source that a cat is drawn to them. The cut must not give a reason. Say "it may want something else in the cake" only if a second-model check allows it, or leave it out.
- SHAKY: Bartoshuk et al. 1971 (Science, abstract only) found cats took sucrose when the water taste was suppressed with salt. It conflicts with the later gene work in tone. See section 4.
- Safety: the ASPCA page and the Merck Veterinary Manual chocolate page were opened. Neither is a government agency. The FDA pages I tried were not found (see "Could not open").
- The main fact is confirmed from a primary source today.

## 2. Three hook facts

1. CS-3 + CS-7: one gene of the two-part sweet receptor is broken, so cats probably cannot taste the sweetness of the cake. Direct from the paper, with its "likely" kept.
2. CS-6: the same broken gene showed up in the one tiger and one cheetah they tested. A big cat with a cake joke, small sample said out loud.
3. CS-10 + CS-11: what cats taste instead is the meaty taste (umami), and the authors guess it is why tuna is so good to them. Hedged as "may" and "we propose".

## 4. Shortened-claim traps

- "Cats can't taste sweet" drops "most likely" and "may be unable". The gene work shows a broken receptor gene and no expression, which is strong. It still stops short of a behavior test in these cats.
- "One gene is broken" is true of Tas1r2, but the sweet receptor needs two proteins. The other, Tas1r3, works. A line that says "cats have no sweet gene" is wrong.
- "Tigers and cheetahs too" needs "the one tiger and one cheetah they tested" (CS-6). The paper also cites older behavior work that tigers and cheetahs "do not prefer sweetened water over plain water".
- "Six cats" is the DNA test. The sex, age and breed of those cats are not in the abstract.
- "Umami is the main taste" is a 2023 proposal from a pet-food company's research arm (CS-10), not a settled textbook fact.
- "Slow blink makes cats like you" overshoots CS-17: 24 cats, one experimenter set, cats more likely to approach, authors say "may" and give two possible explanations.
- Safety: "chocolate is toxic to cats" is not what the two pages opened say word for word. Use "pets" or "many species".

## 5. Disagreements between sources

- Bartoshuk, Harned & Parks 1971 (Science 171:699-701, abstract): "unlike most mammals, cats appear indifferent to sucrose, but this may be because the taste of the sucrose is masked by the taste of the water in which it is dissolved. When the water taste is suppressed by the addition of small amounts of sodium chloride, cats take sucrose avidly." Li et al. 2005 reports no neural response to sucrose in older nerve-recording work and then finds the broken gene. These two do not sit easily together. I could not open the 1971 paper, so I do not know the details. Reason to keep "most likely" and never "flat". Possible reading (mine, not the sources'): the gene work is far more direct, but I cannot prove the 1971 result wrong.
- The 2005 paper's title says "indifference toward sugar", the abstract says "may be unable to detect", the Synopsis says "most likely, cannot detect". The wording shifts inside one paper. Use the hedged one.
- No source I opened gives a reason for begging. Not a disagreement, a gap.
