## 2. Three hook facts

1. CATP1 + CATP2: voice boxes from 8 cats, with no brain attached, still made a purr-low sound (25 to 30 Hz) when air was passed through. 8 of 8. Source: Herbst 2023 abstract, PubMed 37794583.
2. CATP6 + CATP7: cats asking for food slip a cry-like sound into the purr, and people, even non-cat people, rated it as more urgent. Source: McComb 2009 via the Sussex news item. No cat or listener count confirmed.
3. CATP8 + CATP9: a purr is not only a happy sound. Two vet schools (NC State 2012, WSU 2023) say cats may purr when in pain or stressed. Condition: "may", no share of cats.

## 4. Shortened-claim traps

- "Purring is not neural." Wrong when cut. It is "the voice box can do it with no neural input"; the authors say the brain may still add to it.
- "Cats purr when hurt." Wrong when cut. It is "may", from vet descriptions; most purring is not pain.
- "Cats purr to get food." Wrong when cut. It is that a food-asking purr has an extra cry-like sound.
- "8 cats purr without brains." Wrong when cut. It is 8 taken-out voice boxes in a lab, not living cats.
- "Purring is 25 to 30 Hz." Wrong when cut. 25 to 30 Hz is what the excised larynges made; the abstract also cites 20 to 30 Hz as the old theory's muscle rhythm.
- "Experts don't know why." Wrong when cut. They disagree on why (mother-kitten bonding or healing hypothesis); how the sound is made is better understood than why.
- "People find purring annoying." Wrong when cut. Only the food-asking purr was rated more urgent and less pleasant.

## 5. Disagreements between sources

- Frequency: the Herbst abstract says the larynges made 25 to 30 Hz; the EurekAlert release says "20-30 Hz, or cycles per second" for purring-frequency sounds; the abstract cites 20 to 30 Hz as the old muscle-burst rate. Use 25 to 30 Hz (paper), or "around 25 to 30 vibrations a second" and say it is the lab result.
- How purring is made: NC State 2012 says "Purring is triggered by a signal from the central nervous system" (the old model). Herbst 2023 says the larynx can do it with no neural input and the old model is "not fully rejected". The 2023 paper is newer and primary; the 2012 line is a Q&A.
- Why cats purr: WSU lists two ideas and says experts do not agree. The Library of Congress snippet (not opened) says people link it to contentment but cats also purr when injured. None of the pages gives a share of cats.
- McComb author list: the PubMed and Crossref records give McComb, Taylor, Wilson, Charlton (Current Biology 19:R507-R508, 14 July 2009). One search result listed a different author order; use the PubMed record.
