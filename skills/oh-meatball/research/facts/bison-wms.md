## 0. Read this first
- MAIN FACT: CONFIRMED from a primary page today. Yellowstone's Safety page (last updated July 28, 2026, printed on the page) says "Keep at least 25 yards (23 meters) from bison at all times and never approach a bison to take a photo." and "They can run 3x faster than humans". [raw]
- SECOND FACT: CONFIRMED on the same page. Bison "have injured more people in Yellowstone than any other animal". The warning signs are listed (bluff charging, head bobbing while staring, pawing, bellowing, raising its tail). [raw]
- Independent pages opened: Yellowstone Bison Ecology (NPS, last updated May 4, 2026), Wind Cave National Park Safety (NPS, last updated July 26, 2025), Grand Teton Safety (NPS, last updated June 25, 2026), Custer State Park (South Dakota Game, Fish and Parks). All [raw]. Wind Cave is another park from the same agency; Custer is a state agency and gives a different distance.
- The sources DISAGREE on speed (3x a human, 30 mph, 35 mph) and on distance (25 yards at NPS parks, 100 yards at Custer State Park). The script may use "3x faster than humans" only as the Park Service's own words and must not turn it into mph.
- The safety page's advice for a charge is "Immediately walk or run away". This is the OPPOSITE of the bear advice ("Do NOT run"). Never put bear and bison advice in one line.
- Not found: any count of bison injuries per year, for Yellowstone or elsewhere. The pages say "more people than any other animal" and give no number. Do not say a number.

## 2. Three hook facts
1. BISO1 with BISO3: Keep at least 25 yards (23 meters) from bison, and the Park Service says bison have injured more people in Yellowstone than any other animal.
2. BISO2: the Park Service says bison "can run 3x faster than humans" (ratio only, no mph).

## 4. Shortened-claim traps
- "Stay 25 yards away" is true for bison and elk in Yellowstone; five words later it is read as the rule for every animal. Keep "from bison".
- "Run away from a bison" drops the other half of the sentence: "walk or run away", and only when it charges or shows the signs. Safe: "If it charges, walk or run away. Don't stand your ground."
- "Bison hurt the most people" loses "in Yellowstone".
- "Bison run 3x faster" loses "than humans" (and "the Park Service says").
- "Bison charge when the tail goes up" is a cause claim. The page lists the raised tail as one warning sign among several: "may respond".
- "Bear spray works on bison" is not on the page. The page says to spray if it follows you.
- "25 yards" with no conversion: the Park Service gives 23 meters. Keep "yards" or say both. Grand Teton gives 75 feet.
