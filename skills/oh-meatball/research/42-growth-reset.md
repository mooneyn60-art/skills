## 1. Niche: a format niche, with three topic pillars

**Channel promise:** "Meatball, a meatball who's scared of being eaten, explains the weirdest true stuff about the things that might eat him."

YouTube clusters a channel by **who watches**, not by topic labels. The audience stays one audience (curious viewers aged 13-35 who like funny explainers) if every video has the same host, voice, look and packaging.

**Pillars (no fourth until one of these is proven):**
1. **Animals (70% for now).** This is our existing Shorts audience: survival, "would Meatball survive", weird biology.
2. **Food (new, and on-brand).** Meatball *is* food, so food history and weird food facts are where the jokes write themselves. Examples: "Ketchup was sold as medicine", "Why carrots are orange", "The town that banned meatballs".
3. **Weird history and the human body.** Strange true stories, told the way the wolves-to-dogs episode was.

**Expansion rule:**
- A new pillar gets one test long-form plus 3 Shorts.
- It stays only if its Shorts views and its long-form retention are at least as good as the animal average.
- Never run two untested pillars in the same week.

## 2. Thumbnails: the Meatball system

The click comes from **an emotion plus an unanswered question**, and Meatball's face is our recognizable brand in the feed.

**Every thumbnail:**
- **Meatball's face is huge:** 30-40% of the frame, cropped at the chest, with an extreme readable emotion (terror, disgust, shock or smug). He looks **at** the subject, never at the camera.
- **ONE subject:** an animal, a food or an object, cut out with a white outline, big and sharp. It shows something odd or threatening.
- **At most 3 words, or none.** The words don't repeat the title; they open the loop. Example: title "Ketchup Was Once Sold as Medicine", thumbnail text "DOCTOR'S ORDERS?".
- **A visual brand viewers recognize:**
  - one warm saturated background family (sauce-orange to yellow gradient) with a dark vignette
  - the red fork, sauce or arrow device
  - the same font every time
- **Readable at 168 px:** check the mobile-size preview before using it.
- **3 variants per video**, scored with vidIQ `score_thumbnail`. The top two go into YouTube Studio's Test & Compare (Nolan).
- **Banned:** small Meatball in a corner, more than one subject, more than 3 words, quiz framing ("TRUE OR FALSE"), countdown or score text.

## 3. Repeat audience: make it a show people return to

1. **Fixed days:** Tuesday and Saturday at 2 PM, said out loud at the end of every video ("New Meatball every Tuesday and Saturday").
2. **Named series:**
   - "Meatball Explains" (long stories)
   - "Would Meatball Survive?"
   - "Things That Want to Eat Meatball"
   - "Meatball vs Food History"
   - Each series gets its own playlist; playlists drive session time.
3. **Running gags and continuity** (research/39):
   - the Fork that follows him
   - the frying pan that raised him
   - he's always "not a snack"
   - Recurring bits reward returning viewers.
4. **The audience is in the show:**
   - Every episode ends by asking a question and saying the best answer gets read next time.
   - The next episode opens by reading 1-2 commenter names and answers on screen.
5. **Cliffhanger hand-off:** the last 15 seconds tease the next episode. The end screen points to it, and the description links the playlist.
6. **Shorts bridge:** every long-form is cut into 6-12 Shorts, and each Short's "Related video" points to the long-form (Nolan sets this in Studio).
7. **Community tab:**
   - a poll every Wednesday choosing a future topic
   - a Meatball image post every Sunday
   - The Host desk drafts both; Nolan posts them, because no tool can post to the Community tab.
8. **Same identity everywhere:** the same name, avatar and voice on YouTube, TikTok, Instagram and Facebook, so people recognize Meatball wherever they see him.
