# 74. Fact-check: three scheduled long-forms (Night Shift 11/14, 3D House Centipede 11/21, Kill-or-Keep Part 2 11/28), 2026-11-10

Independent second-reviewer check of `scripts/longform-night-shift.json`, `scripts/explainer/house-centipede-longform.json` and `scripts/longform-kill-or-keep-2.json`, plus their description and title files, against the cited sources. Read-only: no script, description, title or schedule file was edited, nothing was committed or posted.

Verdict scale: supported / overstated (source says less) / unsupported by the cited source (another source may support it) / wrong. "Cited" means the source named in the script's `_sources` or `sources`.

How sources were fetched: curl through the proxy for most pages. CDC (bed bugs, rodent cleanup) returned 403 to curl, so I read them through WebFetch. Frontiers in Zoology sits behind a JavaScript bot challenge (curl gets "Client Challenge"), so I read the paper through its Europe PMC copy (PMC11412016, same DOI). PNAS and PeerJ also block curl, so I read their abstracts and full text through Europe PMC (PMC9388130, PMC4157299). Scientific American "Fact or fiction" pages the scripts cite were found by search; the swallow-spiders page loaded directly. Where a cited page 404s or does not contain the claim, I name the other source I used.

Order of attention (publish date): Night Shift first (11/14), then House Centipede (11/21), then Kill-or-Keep 2 (11/28).

## Script 2: 3D House Centipede, "The 30-Legged Bug in Your House You Should Never Kill" (Sat 11/21)

**Claims checked: about 80.** That is 63 Meatball rows (about 60 factual claims), the on-screen cards (48, most repeating the narration), the 3 title candidates, and the description (about 10). Also checked: 12 chapter stamps, 5 source URLs.

Overall: the facts are mostly well sourced. Four need a fix: the title's "Never", the leg-regrowth line (wrong on molts), the "can't tell" absolute, and the bite lines, which leave out the swelling and pain the sources mention.

### Frontiers in Zoology 2024 (leg autotomy), verified

Barutia and Sombke 2024, "Explosive regeneration and anamorphic development of legs in the house centipede Scutigera coleoptrata", DOI 10.1186/s12983-024-00544-0, read via Europe PMC (PMC11412016). Abstract and full text confirm:

- Breaking point: "The trochanter exhibits a preferred breakage point. Internally, it houses a three-layered diaphragm that effectively seals the lumen." Full text: this "allows these animals to easily lose legs in order to escape predators."
- Regeneration is **one molt, not several**: "Centipede appendage regeneration is categorized as 'progressive' or 'explosive'. In the latter, the appendage is fully regenerated after one molt. This term was used for house centipedes." Conclusion: "the genetic predisposition allows them to regenerate legs within one molting cycle."
- Timing caveat: regrowth is "fully regenerated within a single molting cycle (explosive regeneration), if appendotomy occurs before the so-called critical point of the molting cycle, approximately seven days before molting in adult specimens."
- Leg count: "In adults, the 14 pairs of locomotory legs..." plus the ultimate (last) pair = 15 pairs, so the "30-legged" claim holds for adults.
