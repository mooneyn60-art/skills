# 73. Fact-check: House Bug Myths (Thu 11/12) and Every Bug Ranked (Wed 11/18), 2026-11-10

Independent check of `scripts/longform-house-bug-myths.json` and `scripts/longform-house-bugs-ranked.json`, plus their `titles.txt` and `description.txt`, against the cited sources. Read-only: no script, description or title file was edited.

Verdict scale: supported / overstated / unsupported by the cited source / wrong. "Cited" means the URL in the script's `_sources`.

How sources were fetched: curl through the proxy for most pages. CDC (bed bugs, rodents), PeerJ, PNAS, NMNH and Frontiers return 403 or a bot challenge to curl. I read CDC through WebFetch, PeerJ through the PMC copy, and the two papers through their abstracts and press summaries (not the full text). The NMNH page could not be read at all.

### Supported, but worth softening

- Seg 0, L7-L8: "a giant, snoring, breathing earthquake... that's danger". Sci Am: spiders "probably find sleeping humans terrifying". Suggest: "To a spider, that's probably danger."
- Seg 0, L5: "no evidence. Zero." Burke: "no specimens, no eyewitness accounts, nothing". But Sci Am also says people report recovering spider legs and that a swallow "could plausibly" happen. Consider "no solid evidence".
- Seg 1, L8: "nobody's ever shown it's deadly to people." This is accurate. Vetter: no toxicology exists. Consider "and nobody's ever shown it's deadly", which is the same claim.
- Seg 1, L5: "The crane fly... Can't bite." The cited sources don't cover crane flies. It is supported by the Ohio State BYGL and Orange County vector-district pages (crane flies lack piercing mouthparts). Add one to `_sources`.
- Seg 2, L12: "Myth. Your ears are safe." Berenbaum: occurrences are "exceptionally rare... only a single report after surveying about ten centuries of literature", and the same article says cockroaches are the most common insect in ears. Suggest "Myth. Your ears are very safe."
- Seg 2, L5: "Aphids, eggs, rotting plants." UC IPM also says earwigs damage seedlings, soft fruit and flowers. Fine for a house video.
- Seg 3: "Myth. Thirty legs." The 100-legs verdict is fine for house centipedes. More precisely, centipedes have an odd number of leg pairs, so none has exactly 100 legs (Museums Victoria, via search). Optional: "Myth. No centipede has exactly a hundred."
- Seg 3, L4: "Over a foot every second." This rests on a newspaper quoting a UMaine extension diagnostician (16 in/s). The figure is plausible, but a news quote is not a primary measurement.
- Seg 4: "adult... never eats" (UKY: "adults do not feed"), "hates light" (UKY: "avoid light") and "sweaty, stained sweaters most" (UKY and UC IPM: soiled fabrics are preferred) are all supported. "Most" is mild.
- Seg 7, L5: "No brain needed. The body just keeps going." Sci Am (Tipping): ganglia run reflexes, "stand, react to touch and move"; the brain "cannot function normally". Suggest "No brain needed for the basics."
- Seg 6: first-animals framing. Suggest "The first animals launched to space and back alive? Fruit flies."

Verified supported: swallow-spiders myth, vibrations, "spiders want bugs, not your face"; daddy longlegs = 3 animals, harvestman no venom glands, cellar spider venom is "comparatively rather mild" (MDC) and it eats other spiders (MDC); earwig ten centuries / one report, mother guards and feeds young (UC IPM), "might pinch"; 15 pairs = 30 legs, hatch with 4 pairs and gain legs per molt (PSU), leg autotomy (Barutia & Sombke 2024, "preferred breakage point"), prey list (CT: roaches, bed bugs, silverfish); moth eggs 40-50 (UKY and UC IPM), keratin; ballooning posture and electric-field lift; fruit-fly facts (aside from sourcing); headless roach breathing through spiracles, low-pressure open circulation, neck seals by clotting.

Chapters (myths): 0:00, 0:43, 1:19, 1:50, 2:14, 2:43, 3:07, 3:37. All start at 0:00 and are at least 24 s apart. They are **not verified against a render**: there is no mp4 in the folder, and the last chapter implies about 37 s to the 4:13.9 end. Re-check after the render.

## Cross-cutting notes

1. **Black widow / recluse safety language**: R1-R3 are the priority fixes. Ranked is the video that can make a viewer under-react to a widow bite, or over-fear a recluse that is not in their region. Neither description should rely on a generic "see a doctor".
2. **Source hygiene**: wrong or dead citations in public descriptions. Myths: UCR (404), NASA page lacks the 1947 flight, MDC page lacks ballooning. Ranked: PSU (404), UMN (404), NASA page lacks the 1947 flight, NMNH unverifiable. The facts themselves mostly hold; the citations do not.
3. **Consistency across the two videos**: headless roach "a week" (Myths) vs "weeks" (Ranked); fruit flies in space appears in both with the same 1947 claim.
4. **Not checked**: thumbnail images (text only was read from `titles.txt`), the rendered audio, and the mp4 for Myths (not yet rendered).
