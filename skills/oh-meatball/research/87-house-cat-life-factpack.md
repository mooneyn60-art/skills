# Fact pack 87: "Why It Sucks to Be Born as a House Cat" (9 to 11 minute long-form)

Prepared for: Meatball Myths long-form. Research date: 2026-11-12. Chapter prefixes HC-A to HC-I.
Chapters: A where you come from, B your senses, C your food, D your body, E your house, F the vet and the knife, G life outside the house, H the humans, I the other side of the ledger (ending).
Format copied from research 84. Cat facts already checked in research 84 (section CN) are listed in section 11 and not redone.

How each source was opened (the notes column repeats the tag):
- [raw] = I fetched the page, PDF or PMC full text as raw text myself and copied the quote from that text.
- [abstract] = the journal record's abstract, pulled from an API (Europe PMC, OpenAlex or Semantic Scholar). The paper itself was paywalled or not opened, so only the abstract is quoted. Where an abstract is a list of headed parts, I quote one part.
- [relay] = seen only through a search or fetch summary. Nothing in the usable tables rests on [relay] alone. Relay items appear only in the disagreements and not-opened lists, marked UNVERIFIED.
- Quotes keep the source's own spelling. Dates are the year of the paper or page.
- No paywall was bypassed. No mirror copy of a paywalled paper was used. One site (Nature) answered with a cookie redirect; I did not follow it (see section 10).

## Corrections after the second-model check (2026-11-12, research 88). Read before using any row below.

- **HC-D7 and HC-D9 are wrong as written.** Herrera 2026: kidney disease in 80 of 104 staged cats (77%), early stages counted. The 61.3% (49 of 80) is the share of those whose disease was newly found. The 75.6% (34 of 45) is owners reporting no change in urination habits, not no change at all.
- **HC-E6 is misleading as written.** Vnuk 2004: 119 cats; 4 died within 36 hours; 6 were euthanized (2 for prognosis, 4 for cost); 109 lived. The paper's 96.5% counts the euthanized cats as survivors.
- **HC-A12:** the quoted Merck sentence is not on the live page. The page says kittens "handled daily by people during the first month of life have been found to develop more rapidly, be more outgoing, be more social to humans, and have fewer problems with aggression"; the 2 to 7 week sensitive period is on the page.
- **HC-H4:** "cats appear to be exploiting innate tendencies..." is the university news release's own sentence, not a quote from the lead researcher.
- **HC-E12:** the paper does not mention ribbon. Say string and thread.
- **HC-E7:** the call for window barriers is from this Berlin study (1,125 cats), not from the Zagreb series in HC-E6.
- **HC-C9:** six neutered and five intact males; the abstract does not say the 30% is at three months.
- **HC-F7:** the sponsor's memo ("last bone of each toe") was not opened by the checker; use the bill's own words (onychectomy, phalangectomy, tendonectomy).

## 11. The 8 strongest hook facts (ranked)

1. HC-E1: lick a few lily pollen grains off its fur and a cat can get fatal kidney failure in under 3 days; only cats are affected and nobody knows the toxin (FDA). Most surprising, agency's own words, a clean "your home is the threat" beat.
2. HC-G4: in 2,444 UK cats followed for 8 years, road accidents were 45.6% of deaths and 61.2% of kitten deaths. Sets up the indoor versus outdoor trade honestly.
3. HC-H3: stroked by its familiar person, cats showed more negative behaviour (8.5) than with a stranger (5.0); the base of the tail drew the most (Ellis 2015). Counterintuitive; keep the "further investigation" hedge.
4. HC-B2: cats cannot taste sweet; one half of the sweet receptor gene is broken (Li 2005). Clean, short, visual.
5. HC-D9 + HC-D7: in 129 "healthy" cats aged 12 or more, owners missed illness; 75.6% of cats with kidney disease had no change noticed at home (Herrera 2026). Fits "your body betrays you".
6. HC-C1 + HC-C2: normal commercial cat food was linked to heart failure from taurine shortage, and taurine reversed it (Pion 1987, 21 cats).
7. HC-D5: 92% of 100 cats from ages 0 to 20 had X-ray signs of joint disease (Lascelles 2010); say "X-ray signs".
8. HC-E6: 96.5% of 119 cats brought in after window falls survived, but 46.2% had broken limbs; with the selection caveat. The "cats always land on their feet" myth meets data.

## 13. Safety lines that would be wrong if shortened

- Lilies. Shortest true: "True lilies and daylilies can cause fatal kidney failure in cats, even from pollen on fur or water from the vase. Call a vet right away." Dropping "true lilies and daylilies" implies peace lilies kill cats. Dropping "right away" loses the 18-hour limit (HC-E3). Agency words: "Eating just a small amount of a leaf or flower petal, licking a few pollen grains off its fur while grooming, or drinking the water from the vase can cause your cat to develop fatal kidney failure in less than 3 days." (FDA).
- Human medicines. Shortest true: "Never give a cat human pain pills. One extra-strength acetaminophen dose can kill a cat if it is not treated soon." Wrong if cut to "Tylenol is bad": the point is that a person's helpful dose is the danger (the ASPCA article is about exactly that). Agency words: "One extra strength acetaminophen dose can kill a cat if treatment is not started soon after ingestion" (ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center). Do not give a dose or a home remedy; the ASPCA number is on the FDA page as 888-426-4435 (US; may carry a fee, per ASPCA).
- String and thread. Shortest true: "String, thread and ribbon can block a cat's gut and may need surgery." Wrong if it keeps only "all 56 cats survived" (HC-E12). I found no source I could open for "do not pull string out of a cat's mouth": UNVERIFIED, do not say it.
- Windows. Shortest true: "Many cats survive a fall from a window, but many break bones or hurt their chest. Screens and barriers help." Wrong if it keeps only "96.5% survived" (HC-E6). Candela's authors call for "window barriers" (HC-E7).
- Onions and garlic: "Onion, garlic and chives can harm a cat's red blood cells" (no dose given). Tea tree oil: "100% tea tree oil caused serious nerve signs in pets" (HC-E11). Do not say anything about diffusers.
