Checked facts in this video:
- Cubs are born in December or January at about 0.6 kg with their eyes closed, and leave the den at 10 to 12 kg (USGS; Canada's 2008 status report; Environment Canada 2002).
- A mother's fast can exceed 8 months (USGS, 2024), and a pregnant bear may lose as much as 43% of her body mass (Canada's 2008 status report).
- In one 12-year study in the Alaskan Beaufort Sea (published 1995), a cub's chance of surviving its first year was about 65%, and an adult female's chance of surviving each year was about 97%.
- One collared female swam 687 km in 9 days in 2008 (Durner and others 2011).
- Environment Canada (2002) says a bear can detect a seal's breathing hole from up to a kilometre away. We found no source for '20 miles'.
- From 1870 to 2014 there were 73 recorded attacks by wild polar bears on people, with 20 deaths (Wilder and others 2017).
- Manitoba's advice if you meet a polar bear: stay calm, stay with your group, back away facing the bear, do not run, do not play dead, get into a vehicle or a building.
- An estimated 26,000 polar bears worldwide as of 2023 (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service). How they are doing depends on the place: low survival from 2004 to 2006 cut numbers in the southern Beaufort Sea by 25 to 50%, while body condition in Svalbard rose after 2000.
- The verdict at the end is our opinion, not a study's finding. No source we read compares a polar bear's life with any other animal's.
