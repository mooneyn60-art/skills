TITLE: Why the CIA Says "We Can Neither Confirm Nor Deny"

DESCRIPTION:
In 1974 the CIA used a fake deep-sea mining ship, sold to the world as Howard Hughes's, to grab a sunken Soviet nuclear submarine from three miles down. Meatball opens the case file on Project Azorian: the submarine that vanished, the claw, the lift that broke apart, and how "we can neither confirm nor deny" got its name.

WHAT'S CONFIRMED vs DISPUTED
Confirmed by the CIA: K-129 sank in 1968; the Hughes mining story was a cover; the claw ("capture vehicle") and moon pool; the sub broke apart about halfway up; part of it was recovered with the bodies of six Soviet sailors, buried at sea; the 1974 burglary and the 1975 leak; the Glomar response.
Disputed or unconfirmed: how much was recovered (a 38-ft bow section, per the 1998 book Blind Man's Bluff); two nuclear torpedoes (a 1993 Russian report); whether any missile came up (Navy officials quoted by the NYT in 1975 said no); how the US found the wreck (1970s books: Navy underwater microphones); the cost (estimates ~$500 million).
Still classified: what the recovered section held, and what sank K-129.
