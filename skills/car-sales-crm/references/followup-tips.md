# Follow-up tips library

Pull the tip(s) that actually fit the customer's situation — don't paste the same generic line to everyone. Use their notes to pick.

## Creating urgency (use sparingly, only when true)
- Mention real, specific incentives ending soon (rebate deadline, end-of-month/quarter push, limited trim/color in stock) — never invent a deadline that isn't real.
- If a specific unit they liked is genuinely low-stock or a popular trim, mention it plainly: "that color/trim combo doesn't sit long."
- Rate/incentive changes: if financing terms are about to change, that's a legitimate, honest nudge.

## Adding value instead of pushing
- Send something useful, not just "checking in": a payment estimate with their actual trade-in numbers, a comparison of two trims they were between, an answer to a question they asked that you had to look up.
- Reference something specific from their visit/notes — shows you were listening, not sending a form blast.
- If they mentioned a use case (towing, third row, commute length), tie your follow-up directly to that.

## Handling common objections in a follow-up
- **"Need to think about it" / went quiet** → don't re-pitch the car, ask an open question about what's actually holding them up ("was it the payment, or still deciding between the two trims?").
- **Price/payment concern** → come back with real numbers (trade value, different term, different trim), not just "let's make it work."
- **Comparing other dealers** → be straight, don't badmouth competitors; re-state your actual advantage (service, stock, relationship) briefly.
- **Spouse/family needs to weigh in** → offer to send info they can share (specs, payment breakdown, photos) rather than pressuring for another in-person visit right away.

## Channel notes
- **Text**: short, casual, one question or one piece of info, never a wall of text. Best for quick nudges and scheduling.
- **Call**: best when there's something to actually discuss (numbers, a decision). Leave a specific voicemail if no answer — not just "give me a call back."
- **Email**: best for anything with detail attached (numbers, spec comparisons, photos) they'll want to reference later.

## Tone
Sound like a person who remembers them, not a script. Reference specifics from their file. Keep it short — the goal is a reply or a next step, not a full pitch in one message.
